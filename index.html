Behavioral Deception Detection in Instructed LLM Roleplay Is Dominated by Correction-Marker and Instruction-Following Signals: A Three-Control Audit (3B–70B, English)

Anupam Mediratta
Orbis AI  ·  mediratta@gmail.com
View PDF Download PDF

Abstract

Scope: English instructed-roleplay evaluations, open-weight models (3B–70B) only. We present a methodological audit of behavioral LLM deception-detection, applying three evaluation controls (prompt equalization, cross-family feature extraction, and a surface-lexical baseline) to a from-scratch reimplementation of the paradigm exemplified by Pacchiardi et al. (conceptual replication; original code/data not obtained). In our reimplementation, instructed-roleplay accuracies of 93.9–100% collapse to 52–69% under these controls (30–41 pp drop), revealing instruction-following as the dominant signal source. A zero-calibration refusal-count rule (κ ≥ 1) achieves 80.1% pooled-LOO accuracy, matching or exceeding the full 5-feature LLM pipeline on 6 of 7 equalized models. When restricted to the single construct-validated feature (correction-marker density, Krippendorff’s α = 0.606), detection falls to 54.5%, barely above chance. The pipeline’s practical value concentrates at ≥14B multi-turn settings (+14–29 pp over the rule). Semi-autonomous sycophancy shows signal (68–83%) but is largely dispositional; fully-autonomous scenarios yield null results due to scenario-design artifacts (exploratory, n = 10 spot-check). The regimes where detection is validated are precisely where controls reveal artifactual or dispositional signal; the deployment-relevant autonomous regime remains unassessed.
