\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

High accuracy on instructed LLM roleplay does not establish deception-specific detection: such a benchmark identifies $\tau_E$, while the instruction producing the signal also produces the deception---an \emph{identification} failure, not a detector failure. Of ten published rollout sets, nine cannot express the criterion that would attribute a signal to deception; on the tenth the rule finds none in the deployed-observable channel. The field needs benchmarks supplying §\ref{sec:eval_controls}'s five requirements---\textbf{independently validated} deception varying at fixed elicitation---before it needs better detectors.
