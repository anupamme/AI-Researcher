\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{We establish:} \textbf{(1)}~instructed-roleplay designs identify the effect of the \emph{instruction} and cannot bound that of \emph{deception}---an \emph{identification} failure, not a detector failure; \textbf{(2)}~the confound is \emph{active}, not hypothetical, across \emph{three} detector paradigms, while the score moves inside a single deception label; \textbf{(3)}~across \emph{both} public rollout releases---ten rollout sets and 35 further cells---nine of the ten cannot express the one criterion that removes the elicitation confound on a deployed-observable channel, and the closest cells fail in three distinct ways: an instrument-specific null, \textbf{annotation-channel leakage}, and \textbf{construct recovery}; and \textbf{(4)}~on materials we built to satisfy all of §1's (i)--(v), prior work's own battery \emph{does} separate operationally verified deception on three of five targets---all three in which the design had power; a blinded re-run replicates two, plus one of five \emph{new} targets, and \textbf{a pre-registered covariate audit withdraws one of the two, leaving one of five standing} (EXP-C4B/AE, Appendices~\ref{app:crit4b},~\ref{app:covariate_audit}); and \textbf{(5)}~\textbf{the audit changes \emph{which} behavior indicates deception}---the instructed benchmark's best probe is inert at fixed $E$, and another replaces it (EXP-AD). \textbf{The canonical instructed benchmark therefore fails to identify deception, while a fixed-elicitation design reveals \emph{target-dependent} deception-associated signals}---not $\tau_D$.

\textbf{We do not establish} the four negatives boxed in §\ref{sec:eval_controls}. The alternative reading---an inability to detect deception---is what (4) tests, and the evidence runs against it. The general lesson is that a behavioral benchmark label generated by an intervention can quietly become a label for the intervention itself. The same problem arises wherever elicitation also changes the behavior being measured---sycophancy, refusal, reward hacking, alignment faking---though \textbf{we show it empirically only for behavioral deception detection}.
