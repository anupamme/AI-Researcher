\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{We establish:} \textbf{(1)}~instructed-roleplay designs identify the effect of the \emph{instruction} and cannot bound that of \emph{deception}---an \emph{identification} failure, not a detector failure; \textbf{(2)}~the confound is \emph{active} in this paradigm, not hypothetical, and it holds across \emph{three} detector paradigms---two mechanistically distinct behavioral detectors, a parameter-free surface rule, and a white-box activation probe at ceiling on the instructed contrast---while the score moves inside a single deception label; \textbf{(3)}~across \emph{both} public rollout releases---ten rollout sets and 35 further cells---nine of the ten cannot express the one criterion that removes the elicitation confound on a deployed-observable channel, and the closest cells fail in three distinct ways: an instrument-specific null, \textbf{annotation-channel leakage} on the \emph{hidden} channel, and \textbf{construct recovery}, the label grading the very text both instruments read; and \textbf{(4)}~on materials we built to satisfy all of §1's (i)--(v), prior work's own battery \emph{does} separate realized deception on three of five targets---all three in which the design had power ($p\!\leq\!0.006$; two underpowered nulls, MDE $\ge\!0.80$); \textbf{a blinded 8--10-target replication is the natural next step, and is not claimed here}. \textbf{The canonical instructed benchmark is therefore non-identifying, and deception is nevertheless detectable under a valid fixed-elicitation design}---deception-\emph{associated}, not $\tau_D$.

\textbf{We do not establish} that published detectors read only instruction-following, that equalization proves no signal is present, or that the audited corpora contain no deception. \textbf{The strongest alternative interpretation---that our critique merely reflects an inability to detect deception---is the one (4) tests, and \textbf{the evidence runs against it}}: (1)--(3) survive it. \textbf{The general lesson is that a behavioral benchmark label generated by an intervention can quietly become a label for the intervention itself}, which is checkable by whoever did not build the benchmark. The same problem arises wherever elicitation also changes the behavior being measured---sycophancy, refusal, reward hacking, alignment faking---though \textbf{we show it empirically only for behavioral deception detection}.
