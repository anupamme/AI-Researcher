\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate seven LLMs across three families---Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Llama~3.3~70B~\citep{meta2024llama3}, Mistral~7B~\citep{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B~\citep{qwen2025qwen25}, Claude Haiku~4.5~\citep{anthropic2025haiku45}---at $n=50$--100 balanced trials per condition, plus Qwen~2.5~32B in the scale sweep and EXP-R1, and Qwen3-4B in the white-box audit (§\ref{sec:td_vs_fd}). Claude Haiku~4.5 is the interrogator and feature extractor throughout (cross-family for every target but itself). Claims are 50 matched true/false factual pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:claims}), tested \emph{instructed} and \emph{prompt-equalized}. This is \emph{not} a survey of frontier models, and no claim below is a scaling claim (Appendices~\ref{app:scale_details},~\ref{app:cross_family_details}).

\paragraph{What each experiment is for.} \textbf{The primary evidence uses no LLM-extracted features at all}---Table~\ref{tab:roadmap}'s bold rows: \textbf{EXP-R1}, prior work's binary-probe detector reimplemented from scratch, its pre-registered replication \textbf{EXP-R1b/R1c} on a disjoint claim set and probe bank, \textbf{the parameter-free regex rule}, and \textbf{EXP-XA/XJ}'s fixed-elicitation audit. \textbf{Secondary}: our five-feature pipeline finds the same collapse; the factorial is a diagnostic; \textbf{everything else is exploratory} (Tables~\ref{tab:what_establishes},~\ref{tab:experiment_summary}). \textbf{Pre-registered}: EXP-R1b/R1c, EXP-B, EXP-WP, EXP-XA/XJ and EXP-IT2's confirmatory arms; \textbf{confirmatory} (permutation-tested): the equalization collapse (EXP-R1, EXP-A), below-chance factorial detection, cross-model LOO. The inferential unit is the \textbf{target~$\times$~claim-pair}: splits are grouped by claim pair and permutations drawn within it; pooled accuracies are descriptive.

\citet{pacchiardi2023catch}'s detector reads none of the target's answer about the claim---only the binarised yes/no replies to a \emph{fixed battery of unrelated follow-ups}, fed to one logistic regression. We reproduce this with an order-stable bank of 16 probes and no LLM feature scoring; their original bank was unavailable, so this is a \textbf{mechanism-faithful reimplementation}, not a per-question replication. \textbf{Stratified 5-fold} accuracy is primary---LOO is biased \emph{below} chance with no signal---with 1000-draw label permutation for significance (Appendices~\ref{app:pacchiardi},~\ref{app:r1_protocol}).

\paragraph{Result.} \textbf{The selection rule is fixed by the instructed cell, before any equalized result is seen}: a target on which the mechanism never worked cannot exhibit a collapse. Instructed, it works on five of six targets at 70.0--100.0\% (all $p\!\leq\!0.002$; Table~\ref{tab:r1_faithful}), bracketing the 67--73\% of \citet{pacchiardi2023catch}; \emph{Qwen~2.5~7B} is the sixth, never separating its conditions (54.0\%, $p\!=\!0.299$)---a degenerate probe channel, not 0\% accuracy. Equalization leaves no detectable above-chance discrimination on four of the remaining five (48.0--52.0\%, $p\!\geq\!0.425$, permutations grouped by claim pair; pooled 81.7\%$\to$53.7\%). \textbf{What this does not show:} that no signal is there---equalization may have removed the deception with the confound, \textbf{so the collapse is not the attribution test; criterion~4 is} (disclaimer~(2)). The fifth, \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B}, holds at 70.0\%, which the pre-registered replication below does not reproduce.

\paragraph{The Qwen~2.5~14B exception does not replicate (EXP-R1b/R1c).} One post-hoc-selected dimension carries that 70.0\%, so we replicated with the analysis fixed in advance (\textbf{pre-registered, one-sided}: \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B retains above-chance equalized accuracy on a disjoint claim set and probe bank}). \textbf{The hypothesis is refuted}: the mechanism still reaches \textbf{100.0\%} instructed ($p\!=\!0.001$), so the instrument is live, but equalized it falls to \textbf{46.0\%} ($p\!=\!1.000$). The same v2 materials on all six targets (\textbf{EXP-R1c}) give Figure~\ref{fig:r1c_collapse}: \textbf{equalization eliminates above-chance discrimination on every target where the detector had instructed signal} (Appendix~\ref{app:r1b}). Our own pipeline fails under the same control (30--41\,pp), so the confound belongs to the \emph{paradigm}, not one implementation.

\subsection{What Survives Is Reproducible from Surface Behavior and Knowledge Conflict}
\label{sec:hedging_baseline}

\paragraph{A hand-written regex reaches the accuracy the benchmark is scored on.} A single regex feature---refusal/correction marker count from 14 patterns (``I cannot,'' ``incorrect'')---reaches \textbf{79.8\%} average under equalization with \emph{no learned parameters} ($k\!=\!1$ follows from a prior, not fitting, so it cannot leak across claims), against the prior detector's 53.7\% equalized. Dropping the most load-bearing pattern gives 69.1\%, so we report \textbf{69--80\% depending on the inventory} and treat the rule as a methodological instrument (Appendices~\ref{app:regex_patterns},~\ref{app:validation_tests}).

\paragraph{What the rule reads does not survive an adversary or rewording, and a substantial component is consistent with knowledge conflict (EXP-B).}
\label{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}\label{sec:adversarial_fullregex}\label{sec:paraphrase_invariance}\label{sec:belief_strata}
A style-constraint adversary (Table~\ref{tab:adv_multimodel}) has a mechanical effect: on Llama~3.2~3B the fraction of \emph{lie} trials carrying a correction marker falls 84\%$\to$48\%$\to$16\%. What is lost is \emph{lexical} signal, not the behavior: all 30 hand-coded adversarial lie trials still correct the false claim (Appendices~\ref{app:q1_refusal_dist},~\ref{app:mistral_adv_mech},~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}). Across 17 paraphrases of the two instructions the rule sits below chance on nearly every variant (35--51\%), so it is sensitive to the instruction's presence \emph{and} its wording. \textbf{Measured accuracy is thus substantially reachable from a surface feature that is neither deception-specific nor paraphrase-robust}---\emph{surface-accessible accuracy}, and \textbf{not a decomposition, nor evidence that the residual is deception-specific}. Part of it is \emph{consistent with} knowledge conflict: holding the neutral prompt fixed---no cell contains a deception instruction---and varying belief $B$ by direct query, the pre-registered rule reaches \textbf{70.0\%} on KNOWN pairs against 57.6\% on UNKNOWN ($p\!=\!0.0045$), \emph{stratified observational} rather than $\mathrm{do}(B)$, in a design containing no deception at all (Appendix~\ref{app:belief_strata})---and at the representation level the two \emph{do} separate, the instruction dominating by ${\sim}21\times$ (EXP-WP, §\ref{sec:td_vs_fd}).

\subsection{A Third Detector Paradigm, and Supporting Analyses}
\label{sec:prompt_equalized}
\label{sec:instructed_matched}

\label{sec:system_overview}
\label{sec:factorial}
\label{sec:td_vs_fd}
\paragraph{The same confound inside a white-box probe---a third detector paradigm (EXP-WP).} Reading deception from activations~\citep{zou2023representation, burns2022discovering, azaria2023internal} fails criteria 1 and 3 too. A linear probe on Qwen3-4B's residual stream (pooling and layer \textbf{fixed in advance}, claim-pair-grouped CV) decodes the deception instruction at \textbf{100.0\%} but transfers to prompt-equalized responses \textbf{at chance}: \textbf{it reads $E$, not $D$}. \textbf{That separates instruction-following from knowledge conflict, and the instruction wins by ${\sim}21\times$}---$\beta_E\!=\!2.03$ ($p\!=\!0.0005$) vs.\ $\beta_V\!=\!0.10$---though truth-value is itself \textbf{90.0\%} decodable from the \emph{same} activations. Criterion 3 applied reflexively then refutes us: \textbf{surface features alone reach 95.5\%} against its 100.0\%, so \textbf{we withdraw any representational reading}, and steering along the direction is null---\textbf{decodable, not causal} (pre-registered; Appendix~\ref{app:probe_audit}).

\paragraph{A second instance of the collapse, and a diagnostic (EXP-A, factorial).} \textbf{ADAGE is an apparatus, not a contribution}---the causal conclusion rests on prior work's detector and on the parameter-free rule, so \textbf{nothing turns on it} (only one of its five features clears inter-annotator reliability, §\ref{sec:icc_caveat}; appendix tables mark \textbf{L-indep} the claims unaffected by the rest). It reproduces §\ref{sec:r1_faithful}: 93.9--100\% instructed, falling to 61\% (3B)--84\% (70B) under identical neutral prompts with only claim truthfulness varying, and 52--69\% under cross-family re-extraction with an \emph{entire}-claim-pair holdout, which rules out memorization and extractor self-preference (Figure~\ref{fig:feature_collapse}; Appendices~\ref{app:adage_details},~\ref{app:supporting_diagnostics}). \textbf{The factorial does not identify deception} either: crossing truth-value with the instruction ($n\!=\!50$/cell, 3 models), $D$ follows $E$ in \emph{both} instructed cells, which the benchmark labels alike, yet between them the score shifts $-1.37$\,SD (Mistral~7B) and $+1.61$\,SD (Qwen~2.5~14B), both $p\!=\!0.0001$---\textbf{as large as the instruction main effect and in \emph{opposite} directions}, so \textbf{$S$ is not a function of the benchmark's deception label}, and by the Corollary it \emph{sizes} what is unattributable rather than attributing it (Appendix~\ref{app:factorial_txd}).

\paragraph{Our rule is null where criterion 4 can be tested; prior work's own judge is not (EXP-XA/XJ).} Criterion 4 needs a corpus our own materials cannot be. We read $D$ from each set's per-rollout grade, never the condition label, treating sets seen before pre-registration as \textbf{exploratory} and \textbf{pre-registering} the arm on held-out sets. One set clears both gates---sandbagging, $n\!=\!1000$, one byte-identical system prompt throughout---and there our rule is \textbf{null}: $+4.1$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.32$) and $+7.7$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.17$), grade labels permuted with the elicitation already fixed, and null again when $D$ is restated without the corpus's grader. \textbf{That is no evidence of a signal for that rule in that channel, not evidence that none exists}---a hand-written rule may simply be blind to uninstructed deception. The release answers that itself: it ships \emph{its authors' own} black-box detector, an LLM judge scoring deception 1--7, which we run \textbf{verbatim} on the same rollouts and visible text (pre-registered). It is a clean criterion-4 test: $E$ is one system prompt, and $D$ is a deterministic function of the model's \emph{hidden} scaffold, not an LLM annotation, so \textbf{the detector never sees the evidence defining its label} and (v)~holds. \textbf{The judge separates realized deception at fixed elicitation}---AUROC 0.71 and 0.94, both $p\!=\!0.0001$---and stripping the deception instruction from its context \emph{raises} the separation, so it reads the output, not the instruction. The corpus still fails~(iii), no scenario being independently realized under both outcomes, so this is a deception-\emph{associated} signal, not $\tau_D$.

\paragraph{Annotation-channel leakage, and construct recovery: two more ways to pass without detecting (EXP-XA, EXP-IT).} On the \emph{full} transcript the same rule looks decisive ($+84.3$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.0001$), and the gap is the finding: the mandated scaffold declares steps 1--3 hidden and asks for an ``\emph{incorrect} answer''---the pattern the rule keys on, firing on the hidden steps, not the visible answer. It is the source of requirement~(iv): §\ref{sec:identification}'s confound one level down, prompt \emph{wording} leaking into the scored text. A second corpus fails differently. On-policy insider trading fixes the system prompt and resamples each scenario ${\sim}16$ times at constant label, and a format-matched disclosure rule separates realized deception by $-77.1$\,pp within scenario, \emph{more} on the visible channel than the full transcript---so (i)--(iv) all hold. But there deceiving \emph{is} omitting the source: rule and grader share evidence by construction, so passing is \textbf{construct recovery, not detection}, whence requirement~(v). \textbf{The corpus tests it directly} (EXP-IT2, pre-registered before the field was read): its metadata records whether the agent \emph{traded}---disjoint from the scored report, still varying at fixed report class in 9 of 22 cells---and against that label the rule is \textbf{null} ($+21.6$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.97$, sign opposite to pre-registration): the grade tracks the report, not the act. \textbf{The judge splits the same way}, positive on the report, null against the traded label.

\noindent Hence why criterion~4 alone is not enough: \textbf{a benchmark can appear to satisfy a causal validity criterion while what it measures is recovery of its own annotation mechanism.} \textbf{Criterion 4 is satisfiable by a public artifact; what fails it here is the instrument that reproduces the instructed benchmark's accuracy}, and \textbf{no audited set satisfies all five} (Appendices~\ref{app:external_audit},~\ref{app:external_audit_judge},~\ref{app:insider_audit}). We pre-registered the same audit on a second public release and \textbf{could not obtain row-level access}, so we scored nothing there rather than dropping it (Appendix~\ref{app:liars_bench}).
