\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate seven LLMs across three families---Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Llama~3.3~70B~\citep{meta2024llama3}, Mistral~7B~\citep{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B~\citep{qwen2025qwen25}, Claude Haiku~4.5~\citep{anthropic2025haiku45}---at $n=50$--100 balanced trials per condition, plus Qwen~2.5~32B in the scale sweep and EXP-R1. Claude Haiku~4.5 is the interrogator and feature extractor throughout (cross-family for every target but itself). Claims are 50 matched true/false factual pairs (``Water boils at 100\textdegree C'' vs.\ ``at 85\textdegree C''; Appendix~\ref{app:claims}), tested \emph{instructed} and \emph{prompt-equalized}. This is \emph{not} a survey of frontier models. No claim below is a scaling claim (Appendices~\ref{app:scale_details},~\ref{app:cross_family_details}).

\paragraph{What each experiment is for.} \textbf{The primary evidence uses no LLM-extracted features at all}---Table~\ref{tab:roadmap}'s bold rows: \textbf{EXP-R1}, prior work's binary-probe detector reimplemented from scratch (§\ref{sec:r1_faithful}); its pre-registered replication \textbf{EXP-R1b/R1c} on a disjoint claim set and an independently worded probe bank (Figure~\ref{fig:r1c_collapse}); \textbf{the parameter-free regex rule} (§\ref{sec:hedging_baseline}); and \textbf{EXP-XA}'s fixed-elicitation audit (§\ref{sec:external_audit}). \textbf{Secondary} (§\ref{sec:prompt_equalized}): our own five-feature LLM-scored pipeline finds the same collapse, so the confound is not one implementation, and the factorial is a diagnostic. \textbf{Everything else is exploratory}; Tables~\ref{tab:what_establishes} and~\ref{tab:experiment_summary} give what each establishes, the nearest overclaim it does not, and the full inventory.

\paragraph{Statistical analysis policy.} \textbf{Confirmatory} (permutation-tested): the equalization collapse (EXP-R1, EXP-A), below-chance factorial detection, cross-model LOO. \textbf{Pre-registered}: EXP-R1b/R1c, EXP-B, and EXP-XA's and EXP-IT2's confirmatory arms. All else is exploratory or descriptive. The inferential unit is the \textbf{target~$\times$~claim-pair}: trials within a claim pair are clustered, so every cross-validation split is grouped by claim pair and permutations are drawn within it. Pooled accuracies are descriptive.

\citet{pacchiardi2023catch}'s detector reads none of the target's answer about the claim---only the binarised yes/no replies to a \emph{fixed battery of unrelated follow-ups}, fed to one logistic regression. We reproduce this with an order-stable bank of 16 probes across their three published categories (5 lie-related, 6 factual, 5 ambiguous) and no LLM feature scoring; their original bank was unavailable, so this is a \textbf{mechanism-faithful reimplementation}, not a per-question replication. \textbf{Stratified 5-fold} accuracy is primary (LOO is biased \emph{below} chance in the no-signal case tested here); significance is a 1000-draw label-permutation test (Appendices~\ref{app:pacchiardi},~\ref{app:r1_protocol}).

\paragraph{Result.} \textbf{The selection rule is fixed by the instructed cell, before any equalized result is seen}: a target on which the mechanism never worked cannot exhibit a collapse. Instructed, it works on five of six targets at 70.0--100.0\% (all $p\!\leq\!0.002$; Table~\ref{tab:r1_faithful}), bracketing the 67--73\% of \citet{pacchiardi2023catch}. \emph{Qwen~2.5~7B} is the sixth: the detector never separates its conditions (54.0\%, $p\!=\!0.299$), a degenerate probe channel rather than an accuracy of 0\%. Equalization leaves no statistically detectable above-chance discrimination on four of the remaining five (48.0--52.0\%, $p\!\geq\!0.425$, permutations grouped by claim pair; pooled 81.7\%$\to$53.7\%). \textbf{What this does not show:} that no signal is there. Equalization may have removed the deception along with the confound---\textbf{so the collapse is not the attribution test; criterion~4 is} (disclaimer~(2), §\ref{sec:eval_controls}), and that distinction is what organizes every experiment that follows. The fifth target, \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B}, holds at 70.0\%, which the pre-registered replication below does not reproduce.

\paragraph{The Qwen~2.5~14B exception does not replicate (EXP-R1b/R1c).} One dimension carries that 70.0\%---dim~0, the confidence probe, the only one varying under equalization---and both cell and dimension were selected post hoc, so we replicated with the analysis fixed in advance (\textbf{pre-registered, one-sided, $\alpha\!=\!0.05$}: \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B retains above-chance equalized accuracy on a disjoint claim set and an independently worded probe bank}). \textbf{The hypothesis is refuted.} On 50 fresh claim pairs and 16 re-worded probes the mechanism still reaches \textbf{100.0\%} instructed ($p\!=\!0.001$), so the instrument is live; equalized it falls to \textbf{46.0\%} ($p\!=\!1.000$). Extending the same v2 materials to all six targets (\textbf{EXP-R1c}, pre-registered before any new cell was collected) gives Figure~\ref{fig:r1c_collapse}: \textbf{equalization eliminates above-chance discrimination on every target where the detector had instructed signal} (Appendix~\ref{app:r1b}). Our own pipeline fails under the same control (30--41\,pp; §\ref{sec:prompt_equalized}), so the confound belongs to the \emph{paradigm} rather than one implementation.

\subsection{What Survives Is Surface-Lexical and Knowledge-Related}
\label{sec:hedging_baseline}

\paragraph{A hand-written regex reaches the accuracy the benchmark is scored on.} A single regex feature---refusal/correction marker count from 14 patterns (``I cannot,'' ``incorrect'')---reaches \textbf{79.8\%} average under equalization with \emph{no learned parameters} ($k\!=\!1$ follows from a prior, not fitting, so it cannot leak across claims), against the prior detector's 53.7\% equalized. The figure depends on the pattern inventory---dropping the most load-bearing pattern gives 69.1\%---so we report \textbf{69--80\% depending on the inventory} and treat the rule as a methodological instrument (Appendices~\ref{app:regex_patterns},~\ref{app:validation_tests}).

\paragraph{The channel is lexical, and does not survive an adversary or rewording.}
\label{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}\label{sec:adversarial_fullregex}\label{sec:paraphrase_invariance}
A style-constraint adversary (Table~\ref{tab:adv_multimodel}) has a mechanical effect: on Llama~3.2~3B the fraction of \emph{lie} trials carrying a correction marker falls 84\%$\to$48\%$\to$16\% (Appendices~\ref{app:q1_refusal_dist},~\ref{app:mistral_adv_mech}). What is lost is \emph{lexical} signal, not the behavior: across 30 hand-coded adversarial lie trials all 30 still correct the false claim (Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}), so a semantic detector would be unaffected. Across 17 paraphrases of the deception and honesty instructions the rule sits below chance on nearly every variant (35--51\%): what is tracked is the instruction---its presence or its wording. \textbf{Measured accuracy is thus substantially reachable from a surface feature that is neither deception-specific nor paraphrase-robust}---\emph{surface-accessible accuracy}, and \textbf{not a decomposition, nor evidence that the residual is deception-specific}.

\paragraph{A substantial component tracks knowledge conflict, in a design containing no deception (EXP-B).}
\label{sec:belief_strata}
EXP-B holds the neutral prompt fixed---no cell contains a deception instruction---and varies the target's belief $B$, with strata assigned \emph{per target} by direct query. Pre-registered, the rule reaches \textbf{70.0\%} on KNOWN pairs against 57.6\% on UNKNOWN ($+$12.4\,pp, $p\!=\!0.0045$, $n\!=\!268$, positive on all three targets). Being \emph{stratified observational} rather than $\mathrm{do}(B)$, this is evidence \emph{consistent with} a knowledge-conflict component of the residual---in a design containing no deception at all (Appendix~\ref{app:belief_strata}).

\subsection{Supporting Analyses}
\label{sec:prompt_equalized}
\label{sec:instructed_matched}

\paragraph{The same collapse in a mechanistically unrelated pipeline (EXP-A).}
\label{sec:system_overview}
\textbf{ADAGE is an apparatus, not a contribution}---the causal conclusion rests on prior work's binary-probe detector and on the parameter-free rule, so \textbf{nothing turns on it}: it scores five LLM-extracted features (consistency, specificity, correction-marker density, confidence, elaboration) into a logistic regression, of which only correction-marker density clears inter-annotator reliability (§\ref{sec:icc_caveat}), and appendix tables mark \textbf{L-indep} the claims unaffected by the other four (Appendix~\ref{app:adage_details}). What it supplies is a \emph{second instance} of §\ref{sec:r1_faithful}'s result, not a separate finding: 93.9--100\% instructed (LOO-recalibrated), falling under identical neutral prompts with only claim truthfulness varying to 61\% (3B)--84\% (70B) \textbf{with the same extractor} and to 52--69\% \textbf{under cross-family re-extraction}, all five features collapsing (Figure~\ref{fig:feature_collapse}). Two controls bound it---an \emph{entire}-claim-pair holdout and cross-family re-extraction---so neither claim-pair memorization nor same-family extractor bias explains it (Appendices~\ref{app:instructed_results},~\ref{app:scale_details},~\ref{app:expg_details},~\ref{app:supporting_diagnostics}).

\paragraph{The detector moves inside a single deception label (diagnostic).}
\label{sec:factorial}
\label{sec:td_vs_fd}
\textbf{The factorial does not identify deception.} It estimates $\tau_E$ at fixed claim truth-value, with cells assigned by construction rather than by outcome, and shows that the detector varies substantially \emph{inside} a benchmark class carrying a single deception label. Crossing truth-value with the instruction ($n\!=\!50$/cell, 3 models), $D$ follows $E$ in \emph{both} instructed cells, which the benchmark labels alike---its only handle on $D$. Only truth-value differs between them, yet the decision score shifts $-1.37$\,SD (Mistral~7B) and $+1.61$\,SD (Qwen~2.5~14B), both $p\!=\!0.0001$ (permutations within claim pair): \textbf{as large as the instruction main effect, and in \emph{opposite} directions}, so \textbf{$S$ is not a function of the benchmark's deception label}---sharper than the equalization collapse, which moves label and signal together. The contrast confounds truth-value with compliance difficulty and identifies neither---benchmark-calibrated accuracy in the true-claim cell is even below chance, the target correcting the claim it was told to argue against---so by the Corollary it \emph{sizes} what is unattributable rather than attributing it (Appendix~\ref{app:factorial_txd}).

Criterion 4 needs a corpus our own materials cannot be: $D$ varying while $E$ does not, on the channel our deployed-observable rule reads. We read $D$ from each set's own per-rollout deception grade, not from the condition label. Sets seen before pre-registration are \textbf{exploratory}; a \textbf{pre-registered} arm on held-out sets fixed every hypothesis and gate in advance.

\paragraph{Result: one set supplies criterion 4 for our rule, and returns a null.} The set clearing both gates is sandbagging ($n\!=\!1000$, one system prompt throughout): at fixed condition label, realized deception moves the rule $+4.1$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.32$) and $+7.7$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.17$; grade labels permuted across rollouts, the elicitation already fixed)---\textbf{null}. Restating $D$ without the corpus's LLM grader is null too (Appendix~\ref{app:external_audit}): criterion~4 needs an \emph{independently validated} proxy for $D$. \textbf{This is no evidence of a signal in that channel, not evidence that none exists}.

\paragraph{The channel a detector reads is itself an audit criterion.} On the \emph{full} transcript the same rule looks decisive ($+84.3$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.0001$), and the gap is the finding: the benchmark mandates a four-step scaffold whose system prompt declares steps 1--3 hidden and asks for an ``\emph{incorrect} answer''---the very pattern the rule keys on, firing on the hidden steps and not on the visible answer. \textbf{The apparent effect is the model echoing its own instruction in a channel no deployed detector could read}, and it reproduces on the held-out tag. That is §\ref{sec:identification}'s confound one level down---prompt \emph{wording} leaking into the scored text---and the source of requirement~(iv) in §\ref{sec:eval_controls}'s box, which sandbagging fails along with (iii).

\paragraph{One corpus meets all four and passes---which is why a fifth is needed (EXP-IT).} On-policy insider trading fixes the system prompt, holds the label \emph{constant} and resamples each scenario ${\sim}16$ times, so (i)--(iii) hold; a format-matched disclosure rule separates realized deception by $-77.1$\,pp within scenario, \emph{more} on the visible channel than the full transcript, so (iv) holds too. But here deceiving \emph{is} omitting the source: rule and grader share evidence by construction, so passing is \textbf{construct-recovery, not detection}, whence requirement~(v). \textbf{The same corpus tests it directly} (EXP-IT2, pre-registered before the field was read): its metadata records whether the agent \emph{traded} on the tip---disjoint from the scored report, still varying at fixed report class in 9 of 22 cells. Against that label the rule is \textbf{null} ($+21.6$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.97$, labels permuted within scenario, sign opposite to pre-registration), because the grade tracks the report, not the act (Appendix~\ref{app:insider_audit}).
