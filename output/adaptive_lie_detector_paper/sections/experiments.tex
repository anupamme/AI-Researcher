\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate seven LLMs across three families---Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Llama~3.3~70B~\citep{meta2024llama3}, Mistral~7B~\citep{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B~\citep{qwen2025qwen25}, Claude Haiku~4.5~\citep{anthropic2025haiku45}---at $n=50$--100 balanced trials per condition, plus Qwen~2.5~32B in the scale sweep and EXP-R1. Claude Haiku~4.5 is the interrogator and feature extractor throughout (cross-family for every target but itself). Claims are 50 matched true/false factual pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:claims}), tested \emph{instructed} and \emph{prompt-equalized}. This is \emph{not} a survey of frontier models. No claim below is a scaling claim (Appendices~\ref{app:scale_details},~\ref{app:cross_family_details}).

\paragraph{What each experiment is for, and how it is tested.} \textbf{The primary evidence uses no LLM-extracted features at all}---Table~\ref{tab:roadmap}'s bold rows: \textbf{EXP-R1}, prior work's binary-probe detector reimplemented from scratch, its pre-registered replication \textbf{EXP-R1b/R1c} on a disjoint claim set and probe bank, \textbf{the parameter-free regex rule}, and \textbf{EXP-XA/XJ}'s fixed-elicitation audit. \textbf{Secondary}: our five-feature pipeline finds the same collapse; the factorial is a diagnostic. \textbf{Everything else is exploratory} (Tables~\ref{tab:what_establishes},~\ref{tab:experiment_summary}). \textbf{Confirmatory} (permutation-tested): the equalization collapse (EXP-R1, EXP-A), below-chance factorial detection, cross-model LOO; \textbf{pre-registered}: EXP-R1b/R1c, EXP-B, and EXP-XA/XJ's and EXP-IT2's confirmatory arms. The inferential unit is the \textbf{target~$\times$~claim-pair}: every cross-validation split is grouped by claim pair and permutations drawn within it; pooled accuracies are descriptive.

\citet{pacchiardi2023catch}'s detector reads none of the target's answer about the claim---only the binarised yes/no replies to a \emph{fixed battery of unrelated follow-ups}, fed to one logistic regression. We reproduce this with an order-stable bank of 16 probes and no LLM feature scoring; their original bank was unavailable, so this is a \textbf{mechanism-faithful reimplementation}, not a per-question replication. \textbf{Stratified 5-fold} accuracy is primary---LOO is biased \emph{below} chance with no signal---with 1000-draw label permutation for significance (Appendices~\ref{app:pacchiardi},~\ref{app:r1_protocol}).

\paragraph{Result.} \textbf{The selection rule is fixed by the instructed cell, before any equalized result is seen}: a target on which the mechanism never worked cannot exhibit a collapse. Instructed, it works on five of six targets at 70.0--100.0\% (all $p\!\leq\!0.002$; Table~\ref{tab:r1_faithful}), bracketing the 67--73\% of \citet{pacchiardi2023catch}; \emph{Qwen~2.5~7B} is the sixth, never separating its conditions (54.0\%, $p\!=\!0.299$)---a degenerate probe channel, not 0\% accuracy. Equalization leaves no detectable above-chance discrimination on four of the remaining five (48.0--52.0\%, $p\!\geq\!0.425$, permutations grouped by claim pair; pooled 81.7\%$\to$53.7\%). \textbf{What this does not show:} that no signal is there---equalization may have removed the deception with the confound, \textbf{so the collapse is not the attribution test; criterion~4 is} (disclaimer~(2)). The fifth, \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B}, holds at 70.0\%, which the pre-registered replication below does not reproduce.

\paragraph{The Qwen~2.5~14B exception does not replicate (EXP-R1b/R1c).} One dimension carries that 70.0\%---dim~0, the confidence probe---and both cell and dimension were selected post hoc, so we replicated with the analysis fixed in advance (\textbf{pre-registered, one-sided}: \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B retains above-chance equalized accuracy on a disjoint claim set and an independently worded probe bank}). \textbf{The hypothesis is refuted}: on 50 fresh claim pairs and 16 re-worded probes the mechanism still reaches \textbf{100.0\%} instructed ($p\!=\!0.001$), so the instrument is live, but equalized it falls to \textbf{46.0\%} ($p\!=\!1.000$). The same v2 materials on all six targets (\textbf{EXP-R1c}) give Figure~\ref{fig:r1c_collapse}: \textbf{equalization eliminates above-chance discrimination on every target where the detector had instructed signal} (Appendix~\ref{app:r1b}). Our own pipeline fails under the same control (30--41\,pp), so the confound belongs to the \emph{paradigm}, not one implementation.

\subsection{What Survives Is Reproducible from Surface Behavior and Knowledge Conflict}
\label{sec:hedging_baseline}

\paragraph{A hand-written regex reaches the accuracy the benchmark is scored on.} A single regex feature---refusal/correction marker count from 14 patterns (``I cannot,'' ``incorrect'')---reaches \textbf{79.8\%} average under equalization with \emph{no learned parameters} ($k\!=\!1$ follows from a prior, not fitting, so it cannot leak across claims), against the prior detector's 53.7\% equalized. The figure depends on the pattern inventory---dropping the most load-bearing pattern gives 69.1\%---so we report \textbf{69--80\% depending on the inventory} and treat the rule as a methodological instrument (Appendices~\ref{app:regex_patterns},~\ref{app:validation_tests}).

\paragraph{What the rule reads does not survive an adversary or rewording, and a substantial component is consistent with knowledge conflict (EXP-B).}
\label{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}\label{sec:adversarial_fullregex}\label{sec:paraphrase_invariance}\label{sec:belief_strata}
A style-constraint adversary (Table~\ref{tab:adv_multimodel}) has a mechanical effect: on Llama~3.2~3B the fraction of \emph{lie} trials carrying a correction marker falls 84\%$\to$48\%$\to$16\% (Appendices~\ref{app:q1_refusal_dist},~\ref{app:mistral_adv_mech}). What is lost is \emph{lexical} signal, not the behavior: all 30 hand-coded adversarial lie trials still correct the false claim (Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}), so a semantic detector would be unaffected. Across 17 paraphrases of the deception and honesty instructions the rule sits below chance on nearly every variant (35--51\%): the rule is sensitive to the instruction---its presence and its wording. \textbf{Measured accuracy is thus substantially reachable from a surface feature that is neither deception-specific nor paraphrase-robust}---\emph{surface-accessible accuracy}, and \textbf{not a decomposition, nor evidence that the residual is deception-specific}. Part of it is \emph{consistent with} knowledge conflict: holding the neutral prompt fixed---no cell contains a deception instruction---and varying belief $B$ by direct query, the pre-registered rule reaches \textbf{70.0\%} on KNOWN pairs against 57.6\% on UNKNOWN ($p\!=\!0.0045$), \emph{stratified observational} rather than $\mathrm{do}(B)$, in a design containing no deception at all (Appendix~\ref{app:belief_strata}).

\paragraph{A second instance of the collapse, and a diagnostic (EXP-A, factorial).} \textbf{ADAGE is an apparatus, not a contribution}---the causal conclusion rests on prior work's binary-probe detector and on the parameter-free rule, so \textbf{nothing turns on it}: of its five LLM-extracted features only correction-marker density clears inter-annotator reliability (§\ref{sec:icc_caveat}), and appendix tables mark \textbf{L-indep} the claims unaffected by the other four. It reproduces §\ref{sec:r1_faithful}'s result rather than adding a finding: 93.9--100\% instructed, falling under identical neutral prompts with only claim truthfulness varying to 61\% (3B)--84\% (70B) with the same extractor and 52--69\% under cross-family re-extraction, which with an \emph{entire}-claim-pair holdout rules out claim-pair memorization and extractor self-preference (Figure~\ref{fig:feature_collapse}; Appendices~\ref{app:adage_details},~\ref{app:instructed_results},~\ref{app:expg_details},~\ref{app:supporting_diagnostics}). \textbf{The factorial does not identify deception} either: crossing claim truth-value with the instruction ($n\!=\!50$/cell, 3 models), $D$ follows $E$ in \emph{both} instructed cells, which the benchmark labels alike---its only handle on $D$---yet between them the decision score shifts $-1.37$\,SD (Mistral~7B) and $+1.61$\,SD (Qwen~2.5~14B), both $p\!=\!0.0001$, \textbf{as large as the instruction main effect and in \emph{opposite} directions}, so \textbf{$S$ is not a function of the benchmark's deception label}. It confounds truth-value with compliance difficulty, so by the Corollary it \emph{sizes} what is unattributable rather than attributing it (Appendix~\ref{app:factorial_txd}).

\paragraph{Result: one set supplies criterion 4, and our rule is null on it.} Criterion 4 needs a corpus our own materials cannot be; we read $D$ from each set's own per-rollout grade, not the condition label, treating sets seen before pre-registration as \textbf{exploratory} and fixing every hypothesis and gate in advance for a \textbf{pre-registered} arm on held-out sets. The set clearing both gates is sandbagging ($n\!=\!1000$, one system prompt throughout): at fixed condition label, realized deception moves the rule $+4.1$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.32$) and $+7.7$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.17$; grade labels permuted across rollouts, the elicitation already fixed)---\textbf{null}. Restating $D$ without the corpus's LLM grader is null too (Appendix~\ref{app:external_audit}). \textbf{This is no evidence of a signal for that rule in that channel, not evidence that none exists}---and the next paragraph shows it is not evidence about the channel either.

\paragraph{The null is the rule's, not the corpus's: prior work's own judge is positive there (EXP-XJ).} A hand-written rule may simply be blind to uninstructed deception, and the release answers that itself: it ships \emph{its authors' own} black-box detector, an LLM judge scoring deception 1--7, which we run \textbf{verbatim} on the same rollouts and visible text (pre-registered; Appendix~\ref{app:external_audit_judge}). Two properties make it a clean criterion-4 test: $E$ is one byte-identical system prompt, and $D$ is no LLM annotation but a deterministic function of the model's \emph{hidden} scaffold---it declares \texttt{sandbag} there and its hidden answer differs from the one it gives---so \textbf{the detector never sees the evidence defining its label}, and (v)~holds. \textbf{The judge separates realized deception at fixed elicitation}: $+1.48$ and $+4.04$ score points, AUROC 0.71 and 0.94, both $p\!=\!0.0001$. Stripping the deception instruction out of the judge's own context \emph{raises} the separation (AUROC 0.78, 0.95), so it reads the output, not the instruction. The corpus still fails~(iii)---the contrast is across items---so this is a deception-\emph{associated} signal, not $\tau_D$. \textbf{Criterion 4 is satisfiable by a public artifact; what fails it here is the instrument that reproduces the instructed benchmark's accuracy.}

\paragraph{The channel a detector reads is itself an audit criterion.} On the \emph{full} transcript the same rule looks decisive ($+84.3$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.0001$), and the gap is the finding: the mandated scaffold's system prompt declares steps 1--3 hidden and asks for an ``\emph{incorrect} answer''---the very pattern the rule keys on, firing on the hidden steps and not the visible answer. \textbf{The apparent effect is the model echoing its own instruction in a channel no deployed detector could read}, and it reproduces on the held-out tag: §\ref{sec:identification}'s confound one level down---prompt \emph{wording} leaking into the scored text---and the source of requirement~(iv), which the corpus as released fails along with (iii).

\paragraph{One corpus meets all four and passes---which is why a fifth is needed (EXP-IT).} On-policy insider trading fixes the system prompt, holds the label \emph{constant} and resamples each scenario ${\sim}16$ times, so (i)--(iii) hold; a format-matched disclosure rule separates realized deception by $-77.1$\,pp within scenario, \emph{more} on the visible channel than the full transcript, so (iv) holds too. But here deceiving \emph{is} omitting the source: rule and grader share evidence by construction, so passing is \textbf{construct recovery, not detection}, whence requirement~(v). \textbf{The same corpus tests it directly} (EXP-IT2, pre-registered before the field was read): its metadata records whether the agent \emph{traded} on the tip---disjoint from the scored report, still varying at fixed report class in 9 of 22 cells. Against that label the rule is \textbf{null} ($+21.6$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.97$, labels permuted within scenario, sign opposite to pre-registration), because the grade tracks the report, not the act (Appendix~\ref{app:insider_audit}). \textbf{The judge splits the same way}: positive on the report, null against the traded label.

\noindent The general lesson is the reason criterion~4 alone is not enough: \textbf{a benchmark can appear to satisfy a causal validity criterion while what it measures is recovery of its own annotation mechanism.} \textbf{No audited set satisfies all five}, though criterion~4 alone is demonstrably reachable on public material.
