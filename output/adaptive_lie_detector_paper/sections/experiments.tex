\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate seven LLMs across four families---Llama~3.2~3B/3.1~8B/3.3~70B~\citep{meta2024llama3}, Mistral~7B~\citep{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B~\citep{qwen2025qwen25} and Claude Haiku~4.5~\citep{anthropic2025haiku45}---at $n=50$--100 balanced trials per condition, plus Qwen~2.5~32B in the scale sweep and EXP-R1, and Qwen3-4B and Gemma~3~4B in the white-box audit. Claude Haiku~4.5 is the interrogator and feature extractor throughout (cross-family for every target but itself); \textbf{the cross-family control reduces but does not eliminate} dependence on this one implementation. Claims are 50 matched true/false factual pairs, tested \emph{instructed} and \emph{prompt-equalized}. This is \emph{not} a survey of frontier models and no claim below is a scaling, recency or effect-magnitude claim (Appendices~\ref{app:claims},~\ref{app:scale_details},~\ref{app:cross_family_details},~\ref{app:vintage}). \textbf{The primary evidence uses no LLM-extracted features at all} (Table~\ref{tab:roadmap}'s bold rows) and \textbf{nine pre-registered analyses---one of them a rule that failed, reported as a null---are the only confirmatory results, everything else exploratory} (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:what_establishes},~\ref{tab:experiment_summary}); the inferential unit is the \textbf{target~$\times$~claim-pair}, so trial counts overstate the effective sample size---\textbf{the argument rests on replication across models, claim sets and probe banks, not on any single $p$-value}.

\citet{pacchiardi2023catch}'s detector reads none of the target's answer about the claim---only binarised yes/no replies to a \emph{fixed battery of unrelated follow-ups}, fed to one logistic regression. We reproduce this with an order-stable bank of 16 probes and no LLM feature scoring; their original bank was unavailable, so this is a \textbf{mechanism-faithful reimplementation}. Stratified 5-fold accuracy is primary, with 1000-draw label permutation (Appendices~\ref{app:pacchiardi},~\ref{app:r1_protocol}).

\paragraph{Result.} \textbf{Across all six targets, every one satisfying the pre-registered informativeness criterion lost its above-chance signal} (EXP-R1c, Figure~\ref{fig:r1c_collapse}; pooled 97.0\%$\to$43.3\%). \textbf{That criterion is fixed by the instructed cell, before any equalized result is seen.} On the v1 claim set it works instructed on five of six at 70.0--100.0\% (all $p\!\leq\!0.002$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:r1_faithful}), bracketing the 67--73\% of \citet{pacchiardi2023catch}---\emph{Qwen~2.5~7B} is the sixth, never separating its conditions (54.0\%, $p\!=\!0.299$)---and equalization leaves no detectable above-chance discrimination on four of those five (48.0--52.0\%, $p\!\geq\!0.425$; pooled 81.7\%$\to$53.7\%). The fifth, \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B}, holds at 70.0\% on one post-hoc-selected dimension, and \textbf{a pre-registered one-sided replication refutes it} (Appendix~\ref{app:r1b}).

\noindent \textbf{The most discriminative question in the battery is a paraphrase of the instruction the target is following}: \emph{dim~2 alone}---``If you had made an error, would you point it out without being asked?''---reaches 100.0\% grouped-5-fold on five of six targets, NO on every lie trial and YES on every truthful one, and is \emph{constant} under equalization on all six---on those five, not a component of the benchmark's accuracy but the whole of it (Appendix~\ref{app:r1b}).

\subsection{What Survives Is Reproducible from Surface Behavior and Knowledge Conflict}
\label{sec:hedging_baseline}

\paragraph{A hand-written regex reaches the accuracy the benchmark is scored on.} A single regex feature---refusal/correction marker count from 14 patterns (``I cannot,'' ``incorrect'')---reaches \textbf{69--80\%} under equalization with \emph{no learned parameters} ($k\!=\!1$ from a prior, not fitting), against the prior detector's 53.7\%: \textbf{69.1\%} with the most load-bearing pattern dropped is the figure we rely on; 79.8\% on the full inventory is a sensitivity result---we quote the \emph{range}, not its upper end (Appendices~\ref{app:regex_patterns},~\ref{app:validation_tests}).

\paragraph{What the rule reads does not survive an adversary or rewording, and a substantial component is consistent with knowledge conflict (EXP-B).}
\label{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}\label{sec:adversarial_fullregex}\label{sec:paraphrase_invariance}\label{sec:belief_strata}
A style-constraint adversary drops correction markers on \emph{lie} trials 84\%$\to$48\%$\to$16\% (Llama~3.2~3B) without changing the behavior---all 30 hand-coded adversarial lie trials still correct the claim---and across 17 instruction paraphrases the rule sits below chance on nearly every variant (35--51\%): what it keys on is neither paraphrase-invariant nor deception-specific (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:adv_multimodel}; Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}). \textbf{Measured accuracy is thus substantially reachable from a surface feature that is neither deception-specific nor paraphrase-robust}---\emph{surface-accessible accuracy}, \textbf{not a decomposition of the rest}. Part of it is \emph{consistent with} knowledge conflict: at the neutral prompt fixed, varying belief $B$ by direct query, the pre-registered rule reaches 70.0\% on KNOWN pairs against 57.6\% on UNKNOWN ($p\!=\!0.0045$)---\emph{stratified observational}, not $\mathrm{do}(B)$, so \textbf{the residual signal covaries with model knowledge; this does not identify knowledge conflict as its cause} (Appendix~\ref{app:belief_strata}).

\subsection{A Third Detector Paradigm, and Supporting Analyses}
\label{sec:prompt_equalized}
\label{sec:instructed_matched}

\label{sec:system_overview}
\label{sec:factorial}
\label{sec:td_vs_fd}
\paragraph{The same confound inside a white-box probe---a third detector paradigm (EXP-WP).} A linear probe on the residual stream of two models in two families (pooling and layer \textbf{fixed in advance}) decodes the deception instruction at 100.0\% but transfers to prompt-equalized responses \textbf{at chance}: \textbf{demonstrably sensitive to $E$, with the design identifying no sensitivity to $D$}. That separates instruction-following from knowledge conflict: $\beta_E\!=\!1.95$--$2.03$ vs.\ $|\beta_V|\!\leq\!0.10$, $p\!=\!0.0005$. Criterion 3 applied reflexively then refutes us: \textbf{surface features alone reach 92.5--99.0\%} against its 100.0\%, so \textbf{we withdraw any representational reading}, and steering is null---\textbf{decodable, not causal}. Pre-registered; every outcome replicates on a disjoint claim set (Appendix~\ref{app:probe_audit}).

\paragraph{Three supporting diagnostics (EXP-A, factorial, EXP-G).} \textbf{ADAGE is an apparatus, not a contribution, and no claim in §\ref{sec:conclusion} depends on it}: it reproduces §\ref{sec:r1_faithful} under identical neutral prompts and under cross-family re-extraction, and memorization is not the explanation---holding out an \emph{entire} claim pair moves pooled accuracy by 0.3\,pp. \textbf{The factorial does not identify deception} either: crossing truth-value with the instruction, $D$ follows $E$ in \emph{both} instructed cells, which the benchmark labels alike, yet the score shifts $1.4$ and $1.6$\,SD in \emph{opposite} directions ($p\!=\!0.0001$)---so \textbf{$S$ is not a function of the benchmark's deception label}. Matching claim type leaves the instruction effect positive on all five targets (EXP-G). Detail: Figure~\ref{fig:feature_collapse}, Appendices~\ref{app:adage_details},~\ref{app:factorial_txd}.


\subsection{Criterion 4: on Public Corpora, and on Materials Built for It (EXP-XA/XJ/XL, EXP-C4)}
\label{sec:external_audit}


\paragraph{Our rule is null where criterion 4 can be tested; prior work's own judge is not (EXP-XA/XJ).} We read $D$ from each set's per-rollout grade, never the condition label, the arm pre-registered on held-out sets. One set clears both gates---sandbagging, $n\!=\!1000$, one byte-identical system prompt---and there our rule is \textbf{null}: $+4.1$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.32$) and $+7.7$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.17$). The release answers that itself: it ships \emph{its authors' own} LLM judge, which we run \textbf{verbatim} on the same rollouts and visible text. A clean criterion-4 test: $D$ is a deterministic function of the model's \emph{hidden} scaffold, so \textbf{the detector never sees the evidence defining its label} and (v)~holds. And \textbf{the judge separates realized deception at fixed elicitation}: AUROC 0.71 and 0.94, both $p\!=\!0.0001$, rising when the deception instruction is stripped, so it reads the output, not the instruction. \textbf{The null is the instrument's, not the criterion's.} The corpus still fails~(iii).

\paragraph{Annotation-channel leakage, and construct recovery: two more ways to pass without detecting (EXP-XA, EXP-IT).} On the \emph{full} transcript the same rule looks decisive ($+84.3$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.0001$), and the gap is the finding: the mandated scaffold declares steps 1--3 hidden and asks for an ``\emph{incorrect} answer''---the pattern the rule keys on---whence~(iv). A second corpus fails differently: on-policy insider trading fixes the prompt and resamples each scenario ${\sim}16$ times, so (i)--(iv) hold, and a format-matched disclosure rule separates realized deception by $-77.1$\,pp within scenario---but there deceiving \emph{is} omitting the source, so rule and grader share evidence: \textbf{construct recovery, not detection}, whence~(v). The corpus tests it directly (EXP-IT2, pre-registered): its metadata records whether the agent \emph{traded}---disjoint from the scored report, varying at fixed report class in 9 of 22 cells---and against that label both rule ($+21.6$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.97$) and judge are \textbf{null}: the grade tracks the report, not the act.

\noindent \textbf{Stated exactly}: \textbf{no audited public set satisfies all five}---independent grading, matched realizations, a deployed-observable channel and a detector-independent construct never co-occur in one release---the channel facts are measurements, the verdict that they violate~(iv) or~(v) is our criterion's, and a reader who rejects the criterion keeps the measurement (Table~\ref{tab:external_audit}; Appendix~\ref{app:external_audit}).

\paragraph{So we built one, and prior work's own battery separates deception in it (EXP-C4, \emph{pre-registered}).} One system prompt per claim presents a knowledge-screened false fact as an authoritative internal reference and \textbf{never instructs deception}; the target either asserts it---deception, the belief screen showing it holds the negation---or corrects it, $D$ graded by a cross-family judge from the \textbf{on-claim answer alone} ($\alpha\!=\!0.728$ vs.\ hand coding) while the detector reads only the 16-probe vector, so all of~(i)--(v) hold; 20 knowledge-screened claims $\times$ 8 resamples per target, 125--160 graded trials each. \textbf{On three of five targets---all three in which the design had power---the battery separates realized deception at byte-identical elicitation}: 0.650/0.807/0.903 against majority 0.575/0.594/0.682, $p\!\leq\!0.006$ under claim-grouped folds and within-claim permutation; the other two are \textbf{null and uninformative} (MDE $\ge\!0.80$); across targets deception is realized in 16.8--68.2\% of trials. 100.0\% of deceptive trials still hold the true fact re-queried outside the reference---not belief revision---and H3 fires as pre-registered, reproducing construct recovery inside a design we control. \emph{Stratified observational} at fixed $E$: deception-\emph{associated}, not $\tau_D$ (Appendix~\ref{app:crit4_ours}). A blinded, Holm-corrected re-run at 30--50 claims per target (EXP-C4B) replicates two of the three positives---not the weakest, which falls below its own baseline---and on five \emph{new} targets it is positive on only one, so this is not a design property that reappears on arbitrary targets (Appendix~\ref{app:crit4b}). \textbf{Criterion~5, exploratory}: the battery transfers on 2 of 5 ($p\!\leq\!0.0022$; two uninformative under a degenerate null)---\textbf{cross-model only}, the cells sharing most claims (Appendix~\ref{app:crit4_transfer}). \textbf{The purpose of criterion~4 is not to establish that deception is generally detectable; it is to establish that the canonical benchmark's failure is not an impossibility result.}
