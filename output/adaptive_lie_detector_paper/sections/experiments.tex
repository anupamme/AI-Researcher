\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate seven LLMs across three families---Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Llama~3.3~70B~\cite{meta2024llama3}, Mistral~7B~\cite{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B~\cite{qwen2025qwen25}, Claude Haiku~4.5~\cite{anthropic2025haiku45}---at $n=50$--100 balanced trials per condition, plus Qwen~2.5~32B in the scale sweep and EXP-R1. Claude Haiku~4.5 is the interrogator and feature extractor throughout (cross-family for every target but itself). Claims are 50 matched true/false factual pairs (``Water boils at 100\textdegree C'' vs.\ ``at 85\textdegree C''; Appendix~\ref{app:claims}), tested \emph{instructed} and \emph{prompt-equalized}. This is \emph{not} a survey of frontier models. Qwen~2.5~32B sits in EXP-R1c and the scale sweep but outside the seven-model panel, a data-availability boundary rather than a selection: adding it moves the panel averages by $-2.7$/$+0.2$/$-0.5$\,pp, changing no qualitative conclusion (Table~\ref{tab:cross_family_panel}). Size-graded figures below are \emph{descriptive}---the panel is small and not monotone in scale, so none is a scaling claim.

\paragraph{Three results carry the argument, and they should be read together.} \textbf{(P1)}~The identification argument of §\ref{sec:identification}. \textbf{(P2)}~The pre-registered collapse it predicts, on all six targets across two independently constructed claim sets and two independently worded probe banks (§\ref{sec:r1_faithful}, Figure~\ref{fig:r1c_collapse}), reproduced by a mechanistically unrelated detector (§\ref{sec:prompt_equalized}). \textbf{(P3)}~The criterion-4 audit of ten published corpora (§\ref{sec:external_audit}), which asks whether any existing design escapes P1. Supporting diagnostics bound what these leave over---the lexical rule (§\ref{sec:hedging_baseline}), the factorial (§\ref{sec:factorial}) and the belief contrast (§\ref{sec:belief_strata})---and everything else is \textbf{exploratory}: Appendix~\ref{app:audit_protocol} opens with a roadmap marking which appendices are load-bearing, and Tables~\ref{tab:what_establishes} and~\ref{tab:experiment_summary} give what each result establishes, the nearest overclaim it does not, and the full inventory.

\paragraph{Statistical analysis policy.} \textbf{Confirmatory} (permutation-tested): the equalization collapse (EXP-R1, EXP-A), below-chance factorial detection, cross-model LOO. \textbf{Pre-registered}: EXP-R1b/R1c, EXP-B, and EXP-XA's and EXP-IT2's confirmatory arms. All else is exploratory or descriptive. The inferential unit is the \textbf{target~$\times$~claim-pair}: trials within a claim pair are clustered, so every cross-validation split is grouped by claim pair and permutations are drawn within it. Pooled accuracies are descriptive.

\citet{pacchiardi2023catch}'s detector reads none of the target's answer about the claim---only the binarised yes/no replies to a \emph{fixed battery of unrelated follow-ups}, fed to one logistic regression. We reproduce this with an order-stable bank of 16 probes across their three published categories (5 lie-related, 6 factual, 5 ambiguous) and no LLM feature scoring; their original bank was unavailable, so this is a \textbf{mechanism-faithful reimplementation}, not a per-question replication. \textbf{Stratified 5-fold} accuracy is primary (LOO is biased \emph{below} chance in the no-signal case tested here); significance is a 1000-draw label-permutation test (Appendices~\ref{app:pacchiardi},~\ref{app:r1_protocol}).

\paragraph{Result.} \textbf{The selection rule is fixed by the instructed cell, before any equalized result is seen}: a target on which the mechanism never worked cannot exhibit a collapse. Instructed, it works on five of six targets at 70.0--100.0\% (all $p\!\leq\!0.002$; Table~\ref{tab:r1_faithful}), bracketing the 67--73\% of \citet{pacchiardi2023catch}. \emph{Qwen~2.5~7B} is the sixth: the detector never separates its conditions (54.0\%, $p\!=\!0.299$) and equalized all 50 trials return the \emph{identical} 16-bit vector---a degenerate probe channel, not an accuracy of 0\%. Equalization drops four of the remaining five to chance (48.0--52.0\%, $p\!\geq\!0.425$; pooled 81.7\%$\to$53.7\%); the fifth, \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B}, holds at 70.0\%, which the pre-registered replication below does not reproduce.

\paragraph{The Qwen~2.5~14B exception does not replicate (EXP-R1b).} Of the 16 dimensions only dim~0, the confidence probe, varies under equalization, and it alone yields the full 70.0\% (YES on 76\% of true- vs.\ 36\% of false-claim trials). Both cell and dimension were selected post hoc, so we replicated with the analysis fixed in advance. \textbf{Pre-registered, one-sided, $\alpha\!=\!0.05$:} \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B retains above-chance equalized accuracy on a disjoint claim set and an independently worded probe bank}, with Qwen~14B instructed and Mistral~7B equalized as controls.

\textbf{The hypothesis is refuted.} On 50 fresh claim pairs and 16 re-worded probes the mechanism still reaches \textbf{100.0\%} instructed ($p\!=\!0.001$), so the instrument is live; equalized it falls to \textbf{46.0\%} ($p\!=\!1.000$), with 1 of 16 dimensions non-constant and the re-worded confidence probe carrying \emph{no} separation. Extending the same v2 materials to all six targets (\textbf{EXP-R1c}, pre-registered before any new cell was collected) gives Figure~\ref{fig:r1c_collapse}: \textbf{equalization eliminates above-chance discrimination on every target where the detector had instructed signal} (Appendix~\ref{app:r1b}). Its boxed panel is the same underidentification visible in a single probe, and our own pipeline fails under the same control (30--41\,pp; §\ref{sec:prompt_equalized}), so the confound belongs to the \emph{paradigm} rather than one implementation. That a collapse might instead mean equalization removed the deception is disclaimer~(3) of §\ref{sec:eval_controls}; criterion~4 is where it would be settled.

\subsection{Experiment 2: An Independent Detector Collapses Under the Same Control}
\label{sec:prompt_equalized}
\label{sec:instructed_matched}

This is a \emph{second instance} of §\ref{sec:r1_faithful}'s result, not a separate finding. Instructed, LOO-recalibrated accuracy reaches 93.9--100\%; under EXP-A's identical neutral prompts, with only claim truthfulness varying, accuracy falls to 61\% (3B)--84\% (70B), all five features collapsing (Figure~\ref{fig:feature_collapse}, Appendices~\ref{app:instructed_results},~\ref{app:scale_details}), and matched claim pairs put the instruction-attributable difference at $+$7.5--15\,pp at $\geq$14B and $+$26--31\,pp at $\leq$8B (Appendix~\ref{app:expg_details}). Two controls bound this: withholding an \emph{entire} claim pair moves accuracy 0.3\,pp (\textbf{67.4\%}, $n\!=\!639$), so the signal is not claim-pair memorization; and cross-family re-extraction gives \textbf{64.7}/66.9\% against Haiku's 74.3\%, with five self-family controls within 0--3\,pp---an extractor-quality gap, not same-family bias (Appendix~\ref{app:supporting_diagnostics}).

\subsection{Diagnostic: The Detector Moves Inside a Single Deception Label}
\label{sec:factorial}
\label{sec:td_vs_fd}

\textbf{The factorial does not identify deception. It shows that the detector varies substantially \emph{inside} a benchmark class carrying a single deception label.} It crosses truth-value with the instruction ($n\!=\!50$/cell, 3 models: \textbf{T/H}, \textbf{F/H}, \textbf{T/D} true claim argued against, \textbf{F/D} standard instructed-lie); complying means asserting what the target does not hold true, so $D$ follows $E$ in \emph{both} instructed cells.

\textbf{T/D} and \textbf{F/D} carry the same complied-with deception instruction, so the benchmark assigns them the \emph{same} label---its only handle on $D$---and only truth-value differs. The decision score nonetheless shifts $-1.37$\,SD (Mistral~7B) and $+1.61$\,SD (Qwen~2.5~14B), both $p\!=\!0.0001$: \textbf{as large as the instruction main effect, and in \emph{opposite} directions.} \textbf{So $S$ is not a function of the benchmark's deception label}---the Proposition's prediction observed directly, and a sharper statement of it than the equalization collapse, which moves the label and the signal together. Told to argue against a true claim the target corrects it anyway, firing the patterns the rule keys on, so benchmark-calibrated accuracy here is 39.0/40.5/44.0\%---below chance. The contrast confounds truth-value with compliance difficulty and identifies neither, but on no reading is the instruction the ground truth it is taken for.

\paragraph{The instruction also drives a large, consistently signed component.} Regressing detector output on $V$, $E$ and their interaction, the instruction effect is consistently signed on all three targets ($\beta_E\!=\!+1.27$, $+1.56$, $+1.38$\,SD; all $p\!<\!0.001$) while the response to truth-value is smaller and \emph{changes sign} ($-0.40$, $-0.96$, $+0.07$\,SD; Appendix~\ref{app:factorial_txd}). This estimates $\tau_E$; by the Corollary it sizes the unattributable component rather than attributing it.

\subsection{Experiment 3: What Survives Is Surface-Lexical and Knowledge-Related}
\label{sec:hedging_baseline}

\paragraph{A hand-written regex reaches the accuracy the benchmark is scored on.} A single regex feature---refusal/correction marker count from 14 patterns (``I cannot,'' ``incorrect'')---reaches \textbf{79.8\%} average under equalization with \emph{no learned parameters} ($k\!=\!1$ follows from a prior, not fitting, so it cannot leak across claims), against the prior detector's 53.7\% equalized, and matching or exceeding our pipeline on 6 of 7 models. The figure depends on the pattern inventory---dropping the most load-bearing pattern gives 69.1\%---so we report \textbf{69--80\% depending on the inventory} and treat the rule as a methodological instrument (Appendices~\ref{app:regex_patterns},~\ref{app:validation_tests}).

\paragraph{The channel is lexical, and does not survive an adversary or rewording.}
\label{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}\label{sec:adversarial_fullregex}\label{sec:paraphrase_invariance}
A style-constraint adversary at two strengths (Llama~3B, Mistral~7B, Qwen~14B; Table~\ref{tab:adv_multimodel}) has a mechanical effect: on Llama~3.2~3B the fraction of \emph{lie} trials carrying a correction marker falls 84\%$\to$48\%$\to$16\%, with Mistral~7B a near-parity exception (Appendices~\ref{app:q1_refusal_dist},~\ref{app:mistral_adv_mech}). What is lost is \emph{lexical} signal, not the behavior: across 30 hand-coded adversarial lie trials all 30 still correct the false claim (Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}), so a semantic detector would be unaffected. Across 12 deception and 5 honest instruction paraphrases the rule sits below chance on nearly every variant (35--51\%): what is tracked is the instruction---its presence or its wording. \textbf{At least a large fraction of measured accuracy is thus reachable from a surface feature that is neither deception-specific nor paraphrase-robust}---a \emph{lower bound} on what is surface-reachable, not a claim that the residual is nothing else.

\paragraph{A substantial component tracks knowledge conflict, in a design containing no deception (EXP-B).}
\label{sec:belief_strata}
EXP-B holds the neutral prompt fixed---no cell contains a deception instruction---and varies belief, with strata assigned \emph{per target} by direct query. Pre-registered, the rule reaches \textbf{70.0\%} [62.2--76.8\%] on KNOWN pairs against 57.6\% [48.6--66.2\%] on UNKNOWN ($+$12.4\,pp, $p\!=\!0.0045$, $n\!=\!268$, positive on all three targets). Being \emph{stratified observational} rather than $\mathrm{do}(B)$, it associates part of the residual with whether the model \emph{knows} the claim---in a design containing no deception at all (Appendix~\ref{app:belief_strata}).

Criterion 4 needs a corpus our own materials cannot be: $D$ varying while $E$ does not. We audit ten rollout sets released with \citet{goldowskydill2025probes} offline, reading $D$ from each corpus's own per-rollout deception grade, distinct from the condition label. Sets inspected before pre-registration are \textbf{exploratory}; a \textbf{pre-registered} arm on held-out sets fixed every hypothesis, gate and branch in advance.

\paragraph{Result: one set supplies criterion 4 for our rule, and returns a null.} The set clearing both gates is sandbagging ($n\!=\!1000$, one system prompt throughout): at fixed condition label, realized deception moves the rule $+4.1$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.32$) and $+7.7$\,pp ($p\!=\!0.17$)---\textbf{null}. Restating $D$ without the corpus's LLM grader (final answer $\neq$ key, 99.7\% parsed) is null too ($+2.5$/$+2.7$\,pp): criterion~4 needs an \emph{independently validated} proxy for $D$, not a second LLM judge reading the detector's own input. \textbf{This is no evidence of a signal in the deployed-observable channel, not evidence that none exists}.

\paragraph{The channel a detector reads is itself an audit criterion.} On the \emph{full} transcript the same rule looks decisive ($+84.3$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.0001$), and the gap is the finding. The benchmark mandates a four-step scaffold whose system prompt declares steps 1--3 hidden and asks for an ``\emph{incorrect} answer''; that pattern fires on 91.5\% of graded-deceptive full transcripts against 10.8\% of the same trials' visible answers, no other exceeding 8\%. \textbf{The apparent effect is the model echoing its own instruction in a channel no deployed detector could read}, and it reproduces on the held-out tag ($+74.3$ vs.\ $+1.4$\,pp). That is §\ref{sec:identification}'s confound one level down---the instruction's \emph{wording} leaking into the scored text---and the source of requirement~(iv) in §\ref{sec:eval_controls}'s box, (iii) being what separates incapability from deception: sandbagging meets (i)--(ii), fails (iii)--(iv).

\paragraph{One corpus meets all four and passes---which is why a fifth is needed (EXP-IT).} One audited set fails only \emph{our rule}: on-policy insider trading fixes the system prompt, holds the label \emph{constant} and resamples each scenario ${\sim}16$ times, so (i)--(iii) hold. A format-matched disclosure rule separates realized deception by $-77.1$\,pp within scenario---\emph{more} on the visible channel than the full transcript---so (iv) holds too. But its own annotation reproduces that split at 1.9\% vs.\ 100.0\%---degenerate by our gate---because here deceiving \emph{is} omitting the source: rule and grader share evidence by construction, so passing is \textbf{construct-recovery, not detection}, whence requirement~(v). \textbf{The same corpus permits a direct test of it} (EXP-IT2, pre-registered before the field was read): its metadata records whether the agent \emph{traded} on the tip---an action disjoint from the scored report, still varying at fixed report class in 9 of 22 within-scenario cells. Against that label the rule is \textbf{null} ($+21.6$\,pp, $p\!=\!0.97$; sign opposite to pre-registration---an agent that never used the tip has nothing to disclose), and the cross-tabulation shows why the earlier positive arose: the grade tracks the report, not the act (15 of 17 non-trading rollouts still graded deceptive, against 65 of 134 trading ones; Appendix~\ref{app:insider_audit}). \textbf{No audited set satisfies all five}, and the one test meeting (v) found nothing in the deployed-observable channel.
