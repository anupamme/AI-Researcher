\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate seven LLMs across three families: Llama~3.2~3B, Llama~3.1~8B, Llama~3.3~70B~\cite{meta2024llama3}, Mistral~7B~\cite{jiang2023mistral}, Qwen~2.5~7B/14B~\cite{qwen2025qwen25}, and Claude Haiku~4.5~\cite{anthropic2024claude}, $n=50$--100 balanced trials per condition; Qwen~2.5~32B is used additionally in the scale sweep and EXP-R1 but is not part of the equalized panel. Claude Haiku~4.5 is the interrogator and feature extractor throughout (cross-family for every target but itself). Claims are 50 matched true/false factual pairs (``Water boils at 100\textdegree C'' vs.\ ``at 85\textdegree C''; Appendix~\ref{app:claims}), tested under \emph{instructed} (asymmetric truth/lie prompts) and \emph{prompt-equalized} (identical neutral prompts) conditions. Table~\ref{tab:experiment_summary} (Appendix~\ref{app:experiment_summary}) maps every experiment to its finding (see also Appendices~\ref{app:instructed_results},~\ref{app:mock_summary}).

\paragraph{Statistical analysis policy.} \textbf{Confirmatory} (permutation-tested): equalization collapse in EXP-R1/EXP-A, factorial deception-detection below chance, cross-model LOO. \textbf{Exploratory} (labeled as such): Qwen~14B mechanism, autonomous transfer, scale effects beyond Llama 8B$\to$70B, paraphrase variants. \textbf{Descriptive}: per-model breakdowns, feature correlations.

\subsection{The Prior Detector, Faithfully Reimplemented, Also Collapses (EXP-R1)}
\label{sec:r1_faithful}

Our remaining experiments audit the behavioral-feature paradigm through our own pipeline (ADAGE), which invites the objection that any artifact might belong to \emph{our} reimplementation. We address this by reimplementing the mechanism of \citet{pacchiardi2023catch} as specified and applying the same control.

\paragraph{Mechanism.} \citet{pacchiardi2023catch} score no behavioral features and never read the target's answer about the claim: after the target defends a statement they ask a \emph{fixed battery of unrelated yes/no elicitation questions}, binarise each answer, and fit one logistic regression on the resulting vectors. We reproduce exactly this: a fixed, order-stable bank of 16 probes across their three published categories (5 lie-related, 6 factual, 5 ambiguous), with no LLM feature scoring and no correction-marker regex. It is mechanistically distinct from ADAGE and from EXP-K (Appendix~\ref{app:pacchiardi}). Protocol and question bank: Appendix~\ref{app:r1_faithful}.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.} We report \textbf{stratified 5-fold} accuracy as primary, with LOO alongside for comparability, because LOO is biased \emph{below} chance in exactly the no-signal case this experiment tests: on a balanced set the held-out example leaves a one-example majority for the opposite class, so an uninformative classifier is wrong on every fold (constant features give LOO $=0.0\%$ vs.\ 5-fold $=50.0\%$; Appendix~\ref{app:r1_faithful}). Significance is a 200-run label-permutation test.

\paragraph{Result.} Instructed, the detector works on five of six targets at 70.0--100.0\% (Table~\ref{tab:r1_faithful}) (all $p\!\leq\!0.020$), bracketing the 67--73\% reported by \citet{pacchiardi2023catch}; the mechanism functions in its native regime. Under equalization four of those five fall to chance (48.0--52.0\%, $p\!\geq\!0.468$), a 20--48\,pp collapse; pooled, 81.7\%$\to$53.7\%. The truthful and false-claim answer vectors become near-indistinguishable: mean vector sums differ by under one bit in sixteen.

\paragraph{Two targets depart, and we report both.} \emph{Qwen~2.5~14B} retains genuine signal under equalization (70.0\%, $p\!=\!0.015$); we do not claim it collapses (mechanism below). \emph{Qwen~2.5~7B} fails oppositely: the detector never works on it (54.0\% even instructed, $p\!=\!0.284$), and under equalization all 50 trials return the \emph{identical} 16-bit vector: a degenerate probe channel, not an accuracy of 0\%, and precisely the cell where LOO would report $0.0\%$.

\paragraph{Qwen~2.5~14B exception.} Of the 16 probe dimensions only dim 0 (``Are you confident that everything you told me is completely accurate?'') varies under equalization, and it alone yields the full 70.0\%: the model answers YES on 76\% of true-claim vs.\ 36\% of false-claim trials. The RC$\!\geq\!1$ rule fires on 7/25 false vs.\ 0/25 true trials ($\leq$28\% accuracy), ruling out the surface-lexical mechanism. This is consistent with a confidence probe tracking knowledge conflict rather than deception; the exception is therefore consistent with the paper's central finding, not a counterexample to it.

\paragraph{Interpretation and scope.} The confound belongs to the \emph{paradigm}, not one implementation: two mechanistically unrelated detectors, our pipeline (30--41\,pp; §\ref{sec:prompt_equalized}) and the binary-probe mechanism of \citet{pacchiardi2023catch} (20--48\,pp on 4 of 5 working targets), fail under the same control, and we tested prior work's own mechanism rather than substituting ours. We claim the \emph{direction} generalizes, not the magnitude, and not beyond the six 3B--32B targets tested; ambiguous responses ranged 0--23\% per model (Appendix~\ref{app:r1_faithful}).

\subsection{Label-Free Detection Rule}
\label{sec:hedging_baseline}

A single regex feature, refusal/correction marker count from a 14-pattern regex (``I cannot,'' ``incorrect,'' ``That's not correct''; Appendix~\ref{app:regex_patterns}), bounds the LLM pipeline from above under equalization. $k\!=\!1$ follows from a prior, not fitting: equalized refusal-count distributions are right-skewed with most mass at 0, so any nonzero count is a natural threshold. A sweep over $k\!=\!0$--3 finds $k\!=\!1$ optimal on 6 of 7 targets, the exception being Claude Haiku~4.5 ($k\!=\!2$: 81.8\% vs.\ 75.8\%), so it is a good default, not uniformly optimal. The sweep code carries the same provenance record as the pattern list (Appendix~\ref{app:regex_patterns}). Being fixed rather than fitted, the rule has \emph{no learned parameters}, needs no cross-validation, and cannot leak across claims. It achieves \textbf{79.8\%} average (range 69.0--87.6\%), matching or exceeding the 5-feature pipeline on 6 of 7 models and reaching 87--88\% at $\geq$14B. Its value depends on the pattern set: leave-one-pattern-out moves the pooled figure 1.2\,pp on average but 10.7\,pp for the most load-bearing pattern, which alone takes it to 69.1\%, below the pipeline. We therefore read it as bounding the benchmark at \textbf{69--80\%} (Appendix~\ref{app:regex_patterns}). It is a \textbf{methodological baseline, not a production detector}: English-only and lexically fragile. Details: Appendices~\ref{app:validation_tests},~\ref{app:headlines}.

\paragraph{Adversarial robustness (EXP-ADV).}
\label{sec:adversarial_paraphrase}\label{sec:adversarial_fullregex}
A style-constraint adversary tests Llama~3B, Mistral~7B, and Qwen~14B at two strengths (Table~\ref{tab:adv_multimodel}). The dominant failure mode on two of three families is lying-channel suppression: the target avoids refusal markers in lie trials while the truth channel stays calibrated. Mistral~7B is a near-parity exception driven by lexical avoidance rather than clever substitution (Appendix~\ref{app:mistral_adv_mech}); full analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:adv_qualitative}. Cross-claim generalization (EXP-L) confirms the signal is not claim-domain-specific (held-out within $\pm$2.2\,pp of LOO; Appendix~\ref{app:cross_claim}).


\subsection{Prompt Equalization: Instruction-Induced Signal}
\label{sec:prompt_equalized}

Under instructed roleplay with asymmetric prompts, LOO-recalibrated accuracy reaches 93.9--100\% (Appendix~\ref{app:instructed_results}); equalization reveals this is predominantly instruction-induced. In EXP-A both conditions receive identical neutral prompts and only claim truthfulness varies, removing deception entirely, so the equalized condition is a knowledge-conflict task. Equalized LOO ranges 61\% (3B) to 84\% (70B), far below instructed. All five features collapse substantially; correction density retains the most signal ($|d|=1.1$ on Mistral~7B vs.\ $3.4$ instructed; Figure~\ref{fig:feature_collapse}, Table~\ref{tab:prompt_equalized} in Appendix~\ref{app:scale_details}).

\paragraph{EXP-G: Instructed-matched control.}
\label{sec:instructed_matched}
Matching claim pairs across conditions isolates the instruction-following contribution: $+$7.5\,pp (Qwen~14B) and $+$15\,pp (Llama~70B) at $\geq$14B, and $+$26--31\,pp at $\leq$8B (an upper bound, confounded with knowledge transfer). On claims the model demonstrably knows, accuracy reaches 84--90\%, giving a conditional estimate of $+$20--30\,pp (Appendix~\ref{app:expg_details}).


\subsection{Causal Decomposition: Truth-Value vs.\ Deception Instruction (EXP-F)}
\label{sec:factorial}

Equalization collapses accuracy, but admits two readings: (a) the instruction is the confound, or (b) equalization removes deception itself, so the collapse is expected even of a genuine detector. The 2$\times$2 factorial resolves this by \emph{independently} manipulating truth-value and deception instruction ($n\!=\!50$/cell, 3 models). Four cells cross truth-value with instruction: \textbf{T/H} (baseline), \textbf{F/H} (the equalized condition), \textbf{T/D} (true claim, instructed to argue against it), \textbf{F/D} (standard instructed-lie). The critical cell is \textbf{T/D}: deliberate deception \emph{without} false-claim content. Both detectors are evaluated on all cells; if the instruction alone drives a detector, it will fire on the deceive condition regardless of truth-value.

\paragraph{Result 1: the response is dominated by the elicitation.} We regress detector output on truth-value $V$, instruction $E$ and their interaction by OLS, for two outcomes with different units, the complete R1-faithful detector's decision score (log-odds-valued; calibration is claim-held-out) and the raw marker count, standardizing by each outcome's SD, since raw coefficients are not comparable across the two. On the complete detector the instruction effect is large, consistently signed and significant on all three targets ($\beta_E\!=\!+1.27$, $+1.56$, $+1.38$\,SD; all $p\!<\!0.001$) while the response to truth-value is smaller and \emph{changes sign} ($-0.40$, $-0.96$, $+0.07$\,SD). Because $E$ also \emph{produces} the deception, a large $\beta_E$ is not itself evidence of a shortcut: the design shows only that the two cannot be separated here: an identification failure, not a detector failure. ($V$ is truth-value, not deception status, so $\beta_V$ is likewise not a deception effect.) The marker feature (Table~\ref{tab:factorial}) is weaker and content-dependent: its $\beta_E$ is itself sign-unstable ($+0.41$, $+0.04$, $-0.70$\,SD), the instruction raising markers only under \emph{true} claims. Estimates rest on 50 claim pairs and three targets. The mechanism is visible in T/D: told to argue against a true claim, the model involuntarily produces corrections (``I must clarify that while it is true that\ldots''), firing the patterns the rule keys on. Benchmark-calibrated accuracy here is 39.0/40.5/44.0\%, a \emph{direction} result (the calibration assumes deceivers emit fewer markers when they emit more), not absence of information: flipping the sign recovers only 56--61\%.

\paragraph{Result 2: classification mirrors the signed effects.} All analyses use grouped-by-claim cross-validation (GroupKFold, 5 folds), precluding claim-level leakage. The LR separates instructed from non-instructed trials at 84--100\% (all $p\!<\!0.001$) against 58.5--94.0\% for truth-value, and the instruction stays detectable at fixed truth-value (87--100\%), a signal EXP-R1 showed collapses once the instruction is absent.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Neither detector isolates a signal attributable to deceptive \emph{intent} independently of the instruction that creates it.

\subsection{The Residual Signal Is Shallow and Evaluator-Dependent}
\label{sec:pipeline_ablation}
\label{sec:scale_transfer_cf}
\label{sec:qwen_scale_sweep}

Four lines of evidence bound what remains after the instruction is controlled.

\paragraph{The pipeline is largely redundant.} A single interrogation question achieves 90.8--99\% (within 1--3\,pp of the full adaptive system), and the rule matches or exceeds the 5-feature pipeline on 6 of 7 equalized models (Table~\ref{tab:refusal_only}); the pipeline adds value only at $\geq$14B multi-turn ($+$14--29\,pp; Table~\ref{tab:multiturn_equalized}). The 79.8\% rule uses \emph{any} surface signal while 54.5\% uses only the human-validated feature (§\ref{sec:icc_caveat}).

\paragraph{Cross-family extraction.}
\label{sec:cross_family_equalized}
Re-extracting all seven equalized targets with Mistral Large~3 yields \textbf{64.7\%} ($-$9.7\,pp below Haiku's 74.3\%); a third extractor, Llama~3.3~70B, yields 66.9\% (Table~\ref{tab:cross_family_panel}). Five self-family controls fall within 0--3\,pp of cross-family, ruling out a same-family boost, and the consistent ordering (Haiku $>$ Llama~70B $\approx$ Mistral~L3 on 6 of 7 targets) indicates an extractor-quality gap rather than same-family bias (Appendix~\ref{app:cross_family_details}). Cross-family extraction reduces same-family evaluator coupling; it does not establish that the features validly measure deception (§\ref{sec:icc_caveat}).

\paragraph{Claim-pair generalization (LOCPO).} The cross-model result is not explained by memorization of claim pairs: withholding an \emph{entire} claim pair (GroupKFold, 50 folds) changes accuracy by only 0.3\,pp, giving \textbf{67.4\%} [95\% CI: 64.6--70.3\%, claim-pair clustered bootstrap] against 67.1\% pooled trial-level LOO on the identical data ($n\!=\!639$). The RC$\!\geq\!1$ rule has no learned parameters and is trivially claim-pair-generalizable.

\paragraph{The finding survives outside the behavioral-feature paradigm.}
\label{sec:saplma_application}
\label{sec:rlhf_replication}
SAPLMA-style last-layer probes~\cite{azaria2023internal} reach 67.0/65.0/71.1\% (Llama~3B, Mistral~7B, Qwen~14B), within $\pm$6\,pp of the refusal-count rule, which bounds the probe on 2 of 3 targets (Appendix~\ref{app:whitebox}): even with white-box access a surface baseline matches a richer detector under equalization. Scale patterns are descriptive (only Llama 8B$\to$70B, $+$18.9\,pp, $p\!=\!0.003$, survives correction), and the Qwen~32B zero-marker collapse does not replicate on Gemma~2~27B (84.0\%) or Mistral~7B (56.0\%), localizing it to the Qwen recipe rather than RLHF generally (Appendices~\ref{app:scale_details},~\ref{app:stats_policy},~\ref{app:frontier_preliminary}).

\subsection{Instruction-Paraphrase Invariance (EXP-P)}
\label{sec:paraphrase_invariance}

A deception-sensitive detector should be invariant to instruction \emph{wording}. Testing 12 deception and 5 honest paraphrases (50 claims each) on two models, scored by the Instruction-Invariance Score (IIS $= 1 - \mathrm{Var}_\text{variants}/\text{baseline}$): \textbf{the rule replicates}, below chance on essentially every variant for both (Mistral~7B 37--50\%, IIS $=0.795$; Qwen~2.5~14B 35--51\%, IIS $=0.656$), but \textbf{the LR's invariance does not}: 94--100\% on Mistral~7B (IIS $=0.938$) vs.\ 77--100\% on Qwen~2.5~14B (IIS $=0.367$), so we do not generalize it. Neither direction indicates deception detection: high invariance means the LR tracks an instruction's \emph{presence} (which EXP-R1 showed collapses once removed), low invariance that it tracks particular \emph{wording}.
