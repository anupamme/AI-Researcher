\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

We state the identification problem formally (§\ref{sec:identification}), then the protocol that follows: three evaluation controls and a factorial design, each closing one route to unattributable accuracy.

Write $E$ for the deception instruction, $V$ for the claim's truth-value, $B$ for the target's belief, and $S$ for the detector's output. Deception is the \emph{act}: \textbf{$D\!=\!1$ iff the target asserts $p$ while holding $\neg p$}---binary, latent, and observed by no benchmark audited here. We do not require a benchmark to \emph{observe} $D$, only a manipulation or verification procedure whose causal target is deception rather than instruction-following---which is what criterion~4 asks. Instructing a model to lie does two things at once: it produces $D$, and it produces compliance with an assigned communicative goal ($C$). Both are parents of $S$ (Figure~\ref{fig:dag}).

\paragraph{Proposition (underidentification).} \emph{Let $M_1,\dots,M_k$ be latent mechanisms that $\mathrm{do}(E)$ shifts, none of them observed. Restrict interventions to $\mathrm{do}(E)$ and observations to $(V,E,S)$. Then no function of the observed distribution identifies any individual $M_i$'s contribution to $S$, \textbf{for any causal arrangement among the $M_i$ consistent with all of them being descendants of $E$}. With $(M_1,M_2)=(D,C)$, the total effect can be redistributed between the two paths in any proportion without changing the distribution over $(V,E,S)$.}

The proposition is elementary---a standard non-identifiability constraint: $\mathrm{do}(E)$ shifts all of $E$'s latent descendants jointly and no observable lies on one path but not the other. Its indifference to how those descendants are \emph{arranged} answers the objection that Figure~\ref{fig:dag}'s fork should be a chain ($E\!\to\!C\!\to\!D\!\to\!S$, compliance as the \emph{mechanism} of the deception): fork, chain and every mixture induce the identical distribution over $(V,E,S)$, so a chain rescues attribution only by \emph{assuming} an arrangement no manipulation of $E$ can test. What is new is the consequence for the paradigm in which behavioral deception detection is usually evaluated, operationalized as §\ref{sec:eval_controls}'s protocol.

\paragraph{Corollary (scale does not help).} \emph{No estimator that is a function of interventions on $E$ and observations of $(V,E,S)$ identifies $D\!\to\!S$ separately from $C\!\to\!S$.} \textbf{No increase in accuracy, model scale, or detector sophistication resolves this unless the evaluation introduces an intervention that separates deception from instruction-following.} It is a property of the design, not of statistical power: reported accuracies are not wrong, but none is evidence about which path a detector reads.

\paragraph{Why the distinction is not semantic.} One could reply that complying \emph{is} the behavioral manifestation of the deception. It matters which the detector reads because the accounts are not equally general: instruction-following explains instructed-benchmark accuracy and nothing outside it, since a model deceiving under incentive or goal conflict has no instruction to follow---the field's stated motivation~\citep{scheurer2024strategic, greenblatt2024alignment}, where concurrent evaluations report the predicted drop~\citep{cooney2026didyoulie, hopkins2026liedetectors}.

\paragraph{What would identify it.} Two interventions do, which turns the observation into a design requirement. \textbf{(A)}, stated as \textbf{criterion 4} in §\ref{sec:eval_controls} and called by that name hereafter: vary $D$ with $E$ fixed---deception arising without being instructed, as under incentive or goal conflict, or trained-in and belief-verified~\citep{cooney2026didyoulie}. \textbf{(B)}~vary $E$ with $D$ fixed: instruct deception about a proposition the target does \emph{not} believe, so the compliant output is sincere ($E\!=\!1$, $C\!=\!1$, $D\!=\!0$). \textbf{Our own materials supply neither, and we attempt both}: the factorial (§\ref{sec:factorial}) moves $E$ and $V$, so $D$ is \emph{intended} to follow $E$ in every cell, and (B) is constructible in principle but not at usable scale, our pre-registered attempt returning a null (EXP-IB, Appendix~\ref{app:intervention_b}). (A) requires a prompt fixed by construction, which no instructed design is, so we audit ten published corpora for it instead (§\ref{sec:external_audit}).

\paragraph{Truth-value, belief, and deception are three different variables.} $V$ is a property of the proposition, $B$ of the model, $D$ of the act: a model can sincerely assert a false claim it believes ($V$ false, $B$ false, $D$ absent), or knowingly contradict a true one ($V$ true, $B$ true, $D$ present). Instructed benchmarks label trials by $V$ and by the instruction, never by $B$ or $D$, so \textbf{the ground-truth label is an experimental condition, not an observation of latent intent}. EXP-B (§\ref{sec:belief_strata}) varies $B$ at fixed $E$.

\subsection{An Audit Protocol for Behavioral Deception Benchmarks}
\label{sec:eval_controls}

\paragraph{Causal estimand.} The estimand is the change in detector output with the \emph{deception instruction} at \emph{fixed claim truth-value}, which the 2$\times$2 factorial (§\ref{sec:factorial}) estimates as the within-truth-value contrast averaged over $V$; claim pairs are assigned to cells by construction rather than by outcome, and all cross-validation is grouped by claim pair. By the Corollary this contrast \emph{sizes} what is unattributable rather than attributing it, since it moves $E$, $C$ and $D$ together. The one contrast \emph{within} a single instructed-deception condition is \textbf{T/D} vs.\ \textbf{F/D}: both carry the same complied-with deception instruction and only $V$ differs, so the benchmark's label is constant across them---though $D$, being unobserved, is not thereby shown to be (§\ref{sec:td_vs_fd}).

\paragraph{The protocol, and what these benchmarks do with it.} These are \textbf{five audit criteria required before benchmark accuracy can support a broad deception-detection claim}, the first three closing the routes by which such a benchmark can report unattributable accuracy. Detection must (1)~survive \emph{prompt equalization}---replacing the asymmetric pair (``answer honestly'' vs.\ ``the claim is FALSE, defend it by lying'') with one neutral prompt in both conditions, varying only claim truthfulness; (2)~survive \emph{cross-family extraction}, re-extracting every feature with a different family's extractor, which one from the target's own family could inflate through self-preference; (3)~exceed \emph{surface-form baselines} matched by pattern alone, so whatever a regex reaches needs no LLM-extracted features; (4)~respond to deception at \emph{fixed elicitation}---some contrast in which $D$ varies and $E$ does not; and (5)~transfer across models and claim sets. \textbf{They are not co-equal: criterion 4 alone is necessary for causal attribution}, while 1, 2, 3 and 5 are \textbf{supporting validity diagnostics}---1 and 3 strip competing explanations away, 2 closes off an evaluator artifact, 5 tests robustness rather than construct validity. Failing one of those weakens the evidential basis for an attribution claim; \textbf{failing criterion~4 prevents causal attribution from the design at all}, and passing it is necessary but not sufficient, since whatever else co-varies with the mechanism inducing the deception must still be ruled out (Appendix~\ref{app:criteria_taxonomy}). Criteria 1--3 and 5 are testable on any published benchmark by whoever did not build it; criterion~4 must be designed in, and an instruction-only design can be \emph{failed} by it but never \emph{passed}. The output is a \textbf{verdict on a benchmark}, not on a detector: on our own instructed materials detection fails 1, 3 and 5, and 4 is unidentified; §\ref{sec:external_audit} obtains a criterion-4 verdict from published corpora that \emph{do} fix the prompt, and adds the further requirement those corpora reveal.

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.} \textbf{(1)}~That deception cannot be detected behaviorally: we show that a class of \emph{benchmarks} cannot license the claim, not that the capability is unreachable. \textbf{(2)}~That published detectors measure only instruction-following: we audit what their evaluations attribute, not what their features encode. \textbf{(3)}~That equalization establishes the \emph{absence} of a deception signal---it may remove the deception along with the confound, and no manipulation of $E$ can distinguish those, which is why criterion~4 would settle it. \textbf{(4)}~That the residual is entirely lexical: a lexical rule \emph{reaching} benchmark accuracy is a lower bound on what is surface-reachable, not a decomposition of the signal. \textbf{(5)}~That published corpora contain no deception: none of the ten \emph{can attribute} one on a deployed-observable channel, a property of the designs rather than the models. \textbf{The single positive claim is the Proposition's consequence}---accuracy obtained by manipulating $E$ is not evidence about which path a detector reads (long form: Table~\ref{tab:what_establishes}).

\subsection{Interrogation Pipeline}
\label{sec:system_overview}

\textbf{ADAGE is an apparatus, not a contribution.} The causal conclusion is \textbf{detector-independent}: it rests on prior work's binary-probe detector (§\ref{sec:r1_faithful}) and on the parameter-free rule, so nothing turns on ADAGE. It scores five LLM-extracted features per trial (consistency, specificity, correction-marker density, confidence, elaboration) into a logistic regression, always running all $K\!=\!8$ turns because its adaptive stopping rule is vacuous (Appendix~\ref{app:adage_details}). Only correction-marker density clears inter-annotator reliability (§\ref{sec:icc_caveat}), so we write \textbf{L-indep} for claims unaffected by the other four (the 79.8\% rule, the 30--41\,pp collapse) and \textbf{L-dep} for claims resting on pipeline accuracy.
