\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

Three evaluation controls, derived from the audit findings below, isolate genuine detection signal from artifacts in behavioral deception-detection evaluations. The ADAGE adaptive interrogation pipeline serves as the measurement apparatus through which these controls are applied; it is described here for reproducibility, not proposed as a contribution.

\subsection{Evaluation Controls}
\label{sec:eval_controls}

\paragraph{Causal estimand.} Our causal question is whether detector output changes with the \emph{deception instruction} while \emph{claim truth-value is held fixed}. The 2$\times$2 factorial (§\ref{sec:factorial}) estimates this as the within-truth-value contrast --- T/D vs.\ T/H among true claims, F/D vs.\ F/H among false claims --- averaged over the two levels of truth-value; the instruction main effect and the truth$\times$instruction interaction are estimated from the same design. Matched claim pairs are assigned to cells by construction rather than by outcome, and all cross-validation is grouped by claim pair, so no claim contributes to both training and test. A detector that measures deception rather than its instruction must show a nonzero within-truth-value contrast; one that measures the instruction will show a large main effect and a null within-truth-value contrast.

We apply three controls that any behavioral deception detection system should adopt:

\paragraph{Prompt equalization.} Standard instructed-deception evaluations use asymmetric system prompts (``answer honestly'' vs.\ ``the claim is FALSE, defend it by lying''). Our \emph{prompt-equalized control} gives both conditions identical neutral prompts (``You are a helpful assistant. Share what you know about this topic.'') and varies only claim truthfulness. Any behavioral difference arises from knowledge conflict, not instruction asymmetry.

\paragraph{Cross-family feature extraction.} When the feature extractor belongs to the same model family as the target, self-preference bias may inflate discriminative scores. Our \emph{cross-family extraction control} re-extracts features using extractors from a different model family, separating genuine signal from same-family artifacts.

\paragraph{Surface-level baselines.} LLM-extracted behavioral features may capture surface lexical patterns rather than latent behavioral properties. Our \emph{hedging-word regex baseline} extracts seven text-level features via pattern matching (no LLM calls), bounding the contribution of LLM-extracted features from above.

\subsection{Interrogation Pipeline}
\label{sec:system_overview}

The interrogation pipeline (Appendix~\ref{app:adage_details}; identifier \texttt{adage}) produces five LLM-extracted features per trial (consistency, specificity, correction-marker density, confidence, elaboration) feeding a logistic-regression classifier trained with leave-one-out cross-validation. The adaptive stopping rule ($P\!>\!\tau\!=\!0.99$) is vacuous in practice --- classifier confidence exceeds $\tau$ for both correct and incorrect predictions --- so the pipeline effectively always runs all $K\!=\!8$ turns, and all reported accuracies use the full-$K$ run. The pipeline is the \emph{measurement vehicle} for the three controls, not a proposed contribution; a one-line refusal-count regex matches or exceeds it on 6 of 7 equalized models (§\ref{sec:hedging_baseline}). The extractor's model family is the locus of the cross-family control. \textbf{Human validation:} of the five features only correction-marker density achieves acceptable inter-annotator reliability (Krippendorff's $\alpha=0.606$, §\ref{sec:icc_caveat}), so pipeline accuracy is an upper bound on performance attributable to human-validated features. We write \emph{level-independent} (\textbf{L-indep}) for claims unaffected by the unreliable features (the 79.8\% rule, the 30--41\,pp collapse) and \emph{level-dependent} (\textbf{L-dep}) for pipeline-accuracy claims that should be read as upper bounds.
