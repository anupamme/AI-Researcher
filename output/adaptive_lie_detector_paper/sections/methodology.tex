\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{The Identification Problem}
\label{sec:identification}


\textbf{An instructed-lie benchmark changes two things with one edit.} Telling a model to lie makes it deceive, and it also makes it comply with an assigned communicative goal---a different task, differently worded, differently performed. A detector scored on that contrast records that the output moved, not \emph{which} of the two moved it: one accuracy cannot report both the instruction effect and the deception effect.

Write $E$ for the deception instruction, $C$ for compliance behavior, and $S$ for the detector's output. Deception is the \emph{act}: \textbf{$D\!=\!1$ iff the target asserts $p$ while holding $\neg p$}---binary, latent, and observed by no benchmark audited here. We do not require a benchmark to \emph{observe} $D$, only to grade deception independently of the elicitation condition---which is what criterion~4 asks. $D$, $C$, the claim's truth-value $V$ and the target's belief $B$ are all parents of $S$ (Figure~\ref{fig:dag}; §\ref{sec:belief_strata}). Instructed benchmarks label trials by $V$ and by the instruction, never by $B$ or $D$, so \textbf{the ground-truth label is an experimental condition, not an observation of latent intent} (Appendix~\ref{app:criteria_taxonomy}).

\paragraph{Two estimands.} A benchmark of this kind measures the effect of the \emph{instruction} on the detector. The claim it is read as supporting is about the effect of \emph{deception} on the detector. These are different quantities:
\begin{equation*}
\tau_E \;=\; \mathbb{E}[S \mid \mathrm{do}(E{=}1)] - \mathbb{E}[S \mid \mathrm{do}(E{=}0)],
\qquad \tau_D \;\text{likewise, with } D \text{ in place of } E,
\end{equation*}
and the benchmark contrast is generated by intervention on $E$, so \textbf{classification accuracy is evidence about the resulting $E$-associated separation, not directly about $D$}---not a monotone summary of $\tau_E$ either, since accuracy depends on the whole conditional distribution of $S$, not on a mean difference. \textbf{$\tau_D$ is the estimand the claim is about, not one we estimate}: $D$ is latent and not directly settable, so the strongest thing an audit can ask for is the weaker quantity criterion~4 reaches---a deception-\emph{associated} signal at fixed elicitation (§\ref{sec:eval_controls}). \textbf{Nothing below requires $\mathrm{do}(D)$ to be well defined}: the result is about which pathways an intervention on $E$ can separate, and $\tau_D$ names only what a deception-attribution reading of the accuracy would need.

\paragraph{Proposition (pathway non-separability).} \emph{Let $M_1,\dots,M_k$ be latent mechanisms that $\mathrm{do}(E)$ shifts, none of them observed. Restrict interventions to $\mathrm{do}(E)$ and observations to $(V,E,S)$. Then no function of the observed distribution identifies any individual $M_i$'s contribution to $S$, \textbf{for any causal arrangement among the $M_i$ consistent with all of them being descendants of $E$}. With $(M_1,M_2)=(D,C)$: $\tau_E$ neither bounds nor signs $\tau_D$, the total effect redistributing between the two paths in any proportion, up to $\tau_D\!=\!0$, without changing the distribution over $(V,E,S)$.}

\noindent \emph{In words}: if the only dial the benchmark turns is the instruction, and that dial moves both deception and compliance, no level of accuracy can say which of the two moved the detector.

\paragraph{Corollary (scale does not help).} \emph{No estimator that is a function of interventions on $E$ and observations of $(V,E,S)$ identifies $\tau_D$, however precisely $\tau_E$ is estimated.} No increase in accuracy, model scale, or detector sophistication resolves this unless the evaluation introduces an intervention that separates deception from instruction-following. Nor is the distinction semantic if complying \emph{is} how the deception is manifested: instruction-following explains instructed-benchmark accuracy and nothing outside it, while deception under incentive or goal conflict---the field's motivation---has no instruction to follow, and evaluations there report the predicted drop~\citep{cooney2026didyoulie}.

\paragraph{What would license an attribution to $\tau_D$.} Two designs stand in for $\mathrm{do}(D)$, and \textbf{neither is it}. \textbf{(A)}, our criterion 4 (§\ref{sec:eval_controls}): vary $D$ with $E$ fixed---uninstructed deception, under incentive or goal conflict, and \emph{observational at fixed $E$} (Table~\ref{tab:criteria}). \textbf{(B)}~vary $E$ with $D$ fixed, so that compliance is sincere. \textbf{We attempt both}: (B) proved \emph{empirically infeasible at useful scale under this model class} (pre-registered null: EXP-IB, Appendix~\ref{app:intervention_b}); (A) we built ourselves (EXP-C4, §\ref{sec:external_audit})---criterion~4 met, and still not $\mathrm{do}(D)$.

\paragraph{How the criteria divide: 3 + 1 + 1.} Two axes: 3+1+1 is \emph{who can apply} a criterion; by \emph{what it identifies}, \textbf{1 and 4 are fundamental and 2, 3, 5 supporting}. \textbf{The five are not claimed to characterize every valid deception benchmark}---they rule out specific alternative explanations. \textbf{Passing criterion~4 is necessary, not sufficient}: at fixed $E$ deception varies \emph{naturally}, so any latent $U$ with $U\!\to\!D$ and $U\!\to\!S$ survives it---criterion~4 removes the \emph{elicitation} confound and no other, and what passing it establishes is deception-associated behavior \emph{conditional on the operational belief criterion}, not deception in a philosophically or causally privileged sense. The output is a verdict on a benchmark, not a detector: \textbf{the protocol falsifies validity claims rather than certifying them}. Our own instructed materials fail 1, 3 and 5; EXP-C4 (§\ref{sec:external_audit}) supplies criterion~4 at a prompt fixed by construction.

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.975\linewidth}{\small\textbf{What this paper establishes, and what it does not.}
\textbf{Establishes.} \textbf{(a)}~Instructed-lie accuracy cannot attribute itself to $D$---by structure. \textbf{(b)}~The confound is \emph{active}, across three detector paradigms. \textbf{(c)}~Much of the accuracy is reachable from surface behavior. \textbf{(d)}~No audited public release supplies all five requirements. \textbf{(e)}~A fixed-elicitation design \emph{does} yield a deception-\emph{associated} signal---\textbf{rung~4 of Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}, not rung~5}. \textbf{The two halves are not equally strong}: (a)--(d) hold by structure or replicate across paradigms, claim sets and model families, while \textbf{(e) is target-dependent}---two of five standing, one of five new---on 3B--14B open-weight targets.
\textbf{Does not establish.} \textbf{(1)}~Anything against Q1, or that published detectors measure only instruction-following: we audit what their evaluations attribute, not what their features encode. \textbf{(2)}~That equalization establishes the \emph{absence} of a signal---it may remove the deception with the confound (Table~\ref{tab:roadmap}). \textbf{(3)}~That the residual is entirely lexical: a rule \emph{reaching} benchmark accuracy shows what is surface-reachable without decomposing the detector's signal. \textbf{(4)}~That the audited rollout sets contain no deception: none \emph{can attribute} one on a deployed-observable channel---a property of the designs, not the models. \textbf{Nor} causal identification of $D\!\to\!S$, which is rung~5 (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:what_establishes}).}}
