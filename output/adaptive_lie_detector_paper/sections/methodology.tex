\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{The Identification Problem}
\label{sec:identification}


\textbf{An instructed-lie benchmark changes two things with one edit.} Telling a model to lie makes it deceive, and it also makes it comply with an assigned communicative goal---a different task, differently worded, differently performed. A detector scored on that contrast records that the output moved; it does not record \emph{which} of the two moved it. Call these the \textbf{instruction effect} and the \textbf{deception effect}: one accuracy cannot report both.

Write $E$ for the deception instruction, $C$ for compliance behavior, and $S$ for the detector's output. Deception is the \emph{act}: \textbf{$D\!=\!1$ iff the target asserts $p$ while holding $\neg p$}---binary, latent, and observed by no benchmark audited here. We do not require a benchmark to \emph{observe} $D$, only a verification procedure that grades deception independently of the elicitation condition---which is what criterion~4 asks. Both $D$ and $C$ are parents of $S$ (Figure~\ref{fig:dag}), as are the claim's truth-value $V$ and the target's belief $B$, which enter where our experiments vary them (§\ref{sec:belief_strata}). Instructed benchmarks label trials by $V$ and by the instruction, never by $B$ or $D$, so \textbf{the ground-truth label is an experimental condition, not an observation of latent intent} (Appendix~\ref{app:criteria_taxonomy}).

\paragraph{Two estimands.} \textbf{A benchmark of this kind measures the effect of the \emph{instruction} on the detector. The claim it is read as supporting is about the effect of \emph{deception} on the detector.} These are different quantities:
\begin{equation*}
\tau_E \;=\; \mathbb{E}[S \mid \mathrm{do}(E{=}1)] - \mathbb{E}[S \mid \mathrm{do}(E{=}0)],
\qquad \tau_D \;\text{likewise, with } D \text{ in place of } E,
\end{equation*}
and benchmark accuracy is a monotone summary of $\tau_E$. \textbf{$\tau_D$ is the estimand the claim is about, not one we estimate}: $D$ is latent and not directly settable, so $\mathrm{do}(D)$ is a conceptual target rather than an available intervention, and the strongest thing an audit can ask for is the weaker quantity criterion~4 reaches---a deception-\emph{associated} signal at fixed elicitation (§\ref{sec:eval_controls}).

\paragraph{Proposition (underidentification).} \emph{Let $M_1,\dots,M_k$ be latent mechanisms that $\mathrm{do}(E)$ shifts, none of them observed. Restrict interventions to $\mathrm{do}(E)$ and observations to $(V,E,S)$. Then no function of the observed distribution identifies any individual $M_i$'s contribution to $S$, \textbf{for any causal arrangement among the $M_i$ consistent with all of them being descendants of $E$}. With $(M_1,M_2)=(D,C)$: $\tau_E$ is identified and $\tau_D$ is not---$\tau_E$ neither bounds nor signs $\tau_D$, because the total effect can be redistributed between the two paths in any proportion, up to and including $\tau_D\!=\!0$, without changing the distribution over $(V,E,S)$.}

\paragraph{Corollary (scale does not help).} \emph{No estimator that is a function of interventions on $E$ and observations of $(V,E,S)$ identifies $\tau_D$, however precisely $\tau_E$ is estimated.} \textbf{No increase in accuracy, model scale, or detector sophistication resolves this unless the evaluation introduces an intervention that separates deception from instruction-following.} Reported accuracies are not wrong; none is evidence about which path a detector reads. Nor is the distinction semantic if complying \emph{is} how the deception is manifested: instruction-following explains instructed-benchmark accuracy and nothing outside it, while deception under incentive or goal conflict---the field's stated motivation---has no instruction to follow, and evaluations there report the predicted drop~\citep{cooney2026didyoulie, hopkins2026liedetectors}.

\paragraph{What would license an attribution to $\tau_D$.} Two designs stand in for $\mathrm{do}(D)$, and \textbf{neither is it}. \textbf{(A)}, our \textbf{criterion 4} (§\ref{sec:eval_controls}): vary $D$ with $E$ fixed---deception arising uninstructed, under incentive or goal conflict. Being \emph{observational at fixed $E$} it removes the \emph{elicitation} confound and establishes a deception-\emph{associated} signal, whereas $\tau_D$ needs $D$ \textbf{manipulated or randomized}---trained-in and belief-verified~\citep{cooney2026didyoulie}---which no audited design is. \textbf{(B)}~vary $E$ with $D$ fixed, instructing deception about a proposition the target does \emph{not} believe, so compliance is sincere. \textbf{We attempt both and our own materials supply neither}: (B) proved \emph{empirically infeasible at useful scale under this model class} (pre-registered null: EXP-IB, Appendix~\ref{app:intervention_b}), and (A) needs a prompt fixed by construction (§\ref{sec:external_audit}).

\paragraph{How the criteria divide: 3 + 1 + 1 (Table~\ref{tab:criteria}).} \textbf{Criteria 1--3 are diagnostics, each ruling out one alternative explanation} (instruction-following, evaluator artifact, surface behavior---and, at fixed $E$, knowledge conflict); \textbf{criterion~4 is the one construct-validity test}; \textbf{criterion~5 is robustness, and is not equally fundamental}. \textbf{Passing criterion~4 is necessary, not sufficient}: at fixed $E$ deception varies \emph{naturally}, so any latent $U$ with $U\!\to\!D$ and $U\!\to\!S$ survives it---criterion~4 removes the \emph{elicitation} confound and no other. The output is a \textbf{verdict on a benchmark}, not a detector, and \textbf{the protocol falsifies validity claims rather than certifying them}: passing all five leaves a deception-associated signal at fixed elicitation, the causal rung---$D$ manipulated or randomized---sitting outside the protocol by construction. Our own materials fail 1, 3 and 5 with 4 unidentified; §\ref{sec:external_audit} obtains criterion-4 verdicts where the prompt \emph{is} fixed.

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.} \textbf{(1)}~Anything against Q1, or that published detectors measure only instruction-following: we audit what their evaluations attribute, not what their features encode. \textbf{(2)}~That equalization establishes the \emph{absence} of a signal---it may remove the deception with the confound (Table~\ref{tab:roadmap}). \textbf{(3)}~That the residual is entirely lexical: a rule \emph{reaching} benchmark accuracy bounds what is surface-reachable without decomposing it. \textbf{(4)}~That the audited rollout sets contain no deception: none \emph{can attribute} one on a deployed-observable channel---a property of the designs, not the models. \textbf{The positive claim is empirical}: this paradigm instantiates the Proposition's constraint, and the confound in it is active (long form: Table~\ref{tab:what_establishes}).
