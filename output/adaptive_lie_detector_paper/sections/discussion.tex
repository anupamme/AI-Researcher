\paragraph{Scope, generalization, and limitations.}
\label{sec:novelty}

\label{sec:limitations}

Leave-one-model-out yields \textbf{64.2\%} [59--69\%]: the \emph{collapse} replicates across two detectors, two claim sets and two probe banks---the paper's general finding---while the \emph{residual signal} does not transfer across models. \textbf{That non-transfer supports the thesis}: a robust deception representation would transfer; a model-specific behavioral residual is what the identification argument predicts is left over. \textbf{Intervention (B) remains unsupplied} (EXP-IB null, not buildable at scale, Appendix~\ref{app:intervention_b}); criterion 4 is testable only externally; and targets are instruction-tuned $\leq$70B, with larger models and multilingual regimes open (Appendices~\ref{app:loo_across_models},~\ref{app:limitations_detail}).

\paragraph{A structural result and a survey result, which should not be conflated.} \textbf{(i)~Instructed designs identify $\tau_E$ and cannot bound $\tau_D$}---a property of the design, established by the Proposition rather than imposed by us: it holds however the latent mechanisms are arranged, and only a different intervention, not better data, repairs it. \textbf{(ii)~None of ten published corpora meets criterion~4 on a deployed-observable channel with an independently grounded $D$}---a \emph{survey} result about what exists today, and the one we most want falsified (§\ref{sec:eval_controls}'s box). Claim (i): the default protocol is the wrong instrument; claim (ii): nobody has built the right one.
