Leave-one-model-out yields \textbf{64.2\%} [59--69\%]. The \emph{collapse} replicates across two detectors, two claim sets and two probe banks and is the paper's general finding; the \emph{residual signal} does not transfer across models. \textbf{That non-transfer supports the thesis rather than undercutting it}: a robust deception representation would transfer; a model-specific behavioral residual is what the identification argument predicts is left over. \textbf{Intervention (B) remains unsupplied} (EXP-IB is null and could not be built at scale, Appendix~\ref{app:intervention_b}), and criterion 4 is testable only externally. Targets are instruction-tuned $\leq$70B; larger models and multilingual regimes are open (Appendices~\ref{app:loo_across_models},~\ref{app:limitations_detail}).

\paragraph{A structural result and a survey result, which should not be conflated.} \textbf{(i)~Instructed designs cannot attribute their accuracy to deception.} This is a property of the design, established by the Proposition and not a standard we impose: it holds however the latent mechanisms are arranged, and better data cannot repair it---only a different intervention can. \textbf{(ii)~None of ten published corpora currently meets criterion~4 on a deployed-observable channel with an independently grounded $D$.} This is a \emph{survey} result about what exists today, and the one we most want falsified: a corpus that fixes the elicitation, grades $D$ off the detector's own channel, observes each item under both conditions and releases only deployed-observable text would settle in one experiment what no manipulation of $E$ can. Claim (i) says the field's default protocol is the wrong instrument; claim (ii) says nobody has yet built the right one.
