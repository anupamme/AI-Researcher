\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Accuracy on an instructed-lie benchmark does not identify deception-specific signal.} Behavioral deception detection in LLMs almost always reports accuracies in a single setting, \emph{instructed roleplay}: a model is told to lie, and the detector is scored on how well it separates those trials from truthful ones. Two claims must be kept apart. \textbf{(A)}~the detector can classify whether the model is in the instructed-lie condition; \textbf{(B)}~the detector detects deception. \textbf{We do not dispute (A)}---we reproduce it. We show that accuracy in this regime cannot establish \textbf{(B)}, because the instruction is what produces the deception, so the signal's two candidate causes are not separately identifiable from accuracy measured this way. The consequence is the one that matters for deployment: \textbf{such a benchmark establishes no reason for the detector to fire when deception arises by a different causal route}---under incentive or goal conflict, where there is no instruction to comply with (§\ref{sec:identification}). We study the identifiability of \emph{instructed behavioral} deception benchmarks, not deception detection in general.

\paragraph{Contribution.} This paper contributes \textbf{neither a new deception detector nor a new causal-identification theorem}. It contributes (i)~an operational \textbf{audit protocol} for behavioral deception benchmarks---five criteria required before a benchmark's accuracy licenses a claim about deception, four of them applicable to any published benchmark without collecting new data; (ii)~the \textbf{identification result that makes the protocol necessary}, a structural flaw in a widely used experimental paradigm for behavioral LLM deception detection---a standard non-identifiability constraint (§\ref{sec:identification}, Figure~\ref{fig:dag}), deliberately thin, with the work in its consequence; and (iii)~\textbf{an audit under that protocol} of our own instructed materials \emph{and} of ten published rollout sets, showing the paradigm fails it and that this materially changes what published detector results establish. \textbf{The novelty is not the non-identifiability constraint}: it is that the standard evaluation protocol for behavioral LLM deception detection \emph{is itself} an intervention-confounded design, that the failure survives two mechanistically unrelated detectors, two independently constructed claim sets, two independently worded probe banks and a pre-registered replication, and that the constraint yields an operational audit which returns a verdict on corpora we did not build. That verdict is on whether a benchmark \emph{can} attribute its accuracy to deception, not on which factor a given detector reads.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.} White-box probes read deception from activations~\citep{zou2023representation, burns2022discovering, azaria2023internal}, but require weight access; the black-box behavioral line audited here does not. Its closest instance, and our audit target, is \citet{pacchiardi2023catch}: a fixed battery of \emph{unrelated} follow-up questions, binarised and fed to one logistic regression. Three concurrent evaluations stress-test lie detectors at scale---72{,}863 examples over seven datasets~\citep{kretschmar2025liarsbench}, 31 models with 13 belief-verified trained organisms~\citep{cooney2026didyoulie}, and held-out lie categories~\citep{hopkins2026liedetectors}---and agree that detectors fail to \textbf{generalize} beyond the regime they were fit in. Ours is a different question: none isolates the \emph{identifiability} problem created when the intervention that induces deception also induces instruction-following, so none asks whether standard-paradigm accuracy was ever attributable (Appendix~\ref{app:related_work}).

An audit invites an obvious objection---an artifact in the auditors' own reimplementation may say more about the reimplementation than about the paradigm---so we begin with prior work's detector rather than our own (§\ref{sec:r1_faithful}); reimplementation is from scratch throughout, with no original code, data, or question set. Over seven instruction-tuned LLMs from 3B to 70B in three families (§\ref{sec:setup}), the findings are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The prior detector, reimplemented from scratch, also collapses under equalization.} The mechanism of \citet{pacchiardi2023catch} yields 70.0--100.0\% instructed on the five of six targets where it works at all (bracketing their 67--73\%); four fall to chance under equalization (48.0--52.0\%, $p\!\geq\!0.425$; pooled 81.7\%$\to$53.7\%). A pre-registered replication on a disjoint claim set and an independently worded probe bank refutes the exception (Qwen~2.5~14B, 70.0\%$\to$\textbf{46.0\%}, $p\!=\!1.000$) and, extended to all six, finds every one informative and every one at chance (97.0\%$\to$\textbf{43.3\%})---so the collapse holds on \emph{every} target with instructed signal, on \emph{both} claim sets, for two mechanistically unrelated detectors (§\ref{sec:r1_faithful}).
    \item \emph{Diagnostic:} \textbf{a 2$\times$2 factorial sizes what cannot be attributed---and both detectors move within a single instructed-deception condition.} The response to the instruction is large and consistently signed ($+1.3$ to $+1.6$\,SD), that to claim content changes sign; since the instruction produces the deception in every cell, this sizes the unattributable component rather than attributing it. Within one identically-labelled instructed-deception condition the detector still shifts $-1.37$ and $+1.61$\,SD in \emph{opposite} directions (§\ref{sec:td_vs_fd}): either much of this is not deception, or deception varies inside the cell the benchmark calls a lie.
    \item \textbf{What survives is reachable from surface lexical behavior, and a substantial component of it is associated with knowledge conflict.} A hand-specified refusal-count regex reaches \textbf{69--80\%} depending on the pattern inventory: measured accuracy is achievable from a surface feature that is neither deception-specific nor robust to adversarial paraphrasing (§\ref{sec:paraphrase_invariance}). A pre-registered experiment holding the instruction fixed and varying whether the model \emph{knows} the claim reaches \textbf{70.0\%} on claims the target demonstrably knows against 57.6\% on those it does not ($+$12.4\,pp, $p\!=\!0.005$, $n\!=\!268$; §\ref{sec:belief_strata}).
    \item \textbf{None of the ten audited datasets provides all the design properties required to evaluate criterion 4 on a deployed-observable channel.} Of ten rollout sets released with prior work, three vary the elicitation prompt per item, five leave the rule inapplicable, and one has no model outputs. On the single testable set ($n\!=\!1000$, one system prompt throughout) realized deception moves the rule $+4.1$ and $+7.7$\,pp---\textbf{null}, and null again when $D$ is read without the corpus's grader---while scoring that set's \emph{hidden} scaffold instead reports $+84.3$\,pp, effectively all of it one pattern echoing the instruction's own wording, so \textbf{the channel a detector reads is itself an audit criterion}. A second corpus meets all four and \emph{passes} ($-77.1$\,pp, exploratory), but grader and rule there share evidence by construction, so passing is \textbf{construct-recovery, not detection}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope.} The setting is \textbf{instructed roleplay on open-weight models (3B--70B)}, English only, with six 3B--32B targets and one claim set for the reimplementation. Autonomous strategic deception~\cite{hubinger2019risks, scheurer2024strategic, greenblatt2024alignment}---the regime criterion 4 requires---enters only as the external audit of §\ref{sec:external_audit}, on corpora we did not build.

\paragraph{Why this matters beyond deception.} The failure mode is not specific to deception. Whenever a benchmark's intervention simultaneously changes the target construct \emph{and} an auxiliary behavior the evaluator reads, predictive accuracy identifies the benchmark condition rather than the construct, and no amount of accuracy repairs it. Instructed sycophancy, refusal, reward hacking and alignment faking are all elicited this way and share the design. \textbf{We test only deception}; what we offer for the rest is the audit---a procedure for asking, of any such benchmark, which intervention produced the difference being scored.
