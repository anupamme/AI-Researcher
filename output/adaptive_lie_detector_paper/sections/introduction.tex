\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Behavioral safety benchmarks are validated by predictive accuracy, not by construct identification. We introduce an identification audit for such benchmarks, and show that the dominant LLM deception paradigm fails it.} \textbf{We do not ask whether LLMs can detect deception; we ask whether current benchmarks provide evidence that they do}---and \textbf{accuracy on an instructed-lie benchmark cannot, by itself, establish that a detector responds to deception rather than to behaviors induced by the deception instruction}. A common paradigm reports accuracies in one setting, \emph{instructed roleplay}: a model is told to lie, and the detector scored on separating those trials from truthful ones. Because the instruction produces the deception, the signal's two candidate causes are not separately identifiable that way. \textbf{We do not claim that deception is undetectable}---we end by detecting it under a design that removes the confound (§\ref{sec:external_audit}). \textbf{Nor do we estimate $\tau_D$}, the effect of deception on the detector: it is introduced only as the causal quantity the benchmark's interpretation implicitly invokes. $D$ is latent throughout the instructed paradigm, ours included; the point is that manipulating only the instruction cannot identify it from the condition manipulated. \textbf{Our contribution is not a new causal-identification theorem; it is the empirical demonstration that a widely used LLM deception evaluation protocol instantiates a known non-identifying design.}

\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.975\linewidth}{\small\textbf{Three questions, kept apart.}
\textbf{Q1. Can a detector classify instructed-lie trials against truthful ones?} \textbf{Yes}---we reproduce it and dispute none of it.
\textbf{Q2. Does that signal arise from the deception rather than from following the instruction?} \textbf{Unidentified}---the instruction moves both, so no accuracy measured this way separates them (§\ref{sec:identification}).
\textbf{Q3. Will the detector fire when deception arises with no instruction to comply with}, under incentive or goal conflict? \textbf{Not for the audited surface rule}, but prior work's probe battery \emph{does} separate deception on materials we built to make that test valid (§\ref{sec:external_audit}).
This paper is about \textbf{Q2 and Q3}: an \emph{identification} claim about a class of benchmarks, not a capability claim about detectors.}}

\paragraph{Contribution: four claims, in descending order of how strongly they are warranted (Table~\ref{tab:roadmap}; Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} shows how far up the evidence ladder they reach).} \textbf{We formalize the benchmark's estimand mismatch, turn it into an operational five-criterion audit, show the confound across three detector paradigms, and audit the public rollout corpora for the missing fixed-elicitation contrast.} \textbf{(1)~Framework.} Instructed benchmarks identify $\tau_E$, the \emph{instruction}'s effect, while deception-detection claims are about $\tau_D$ (§\ref{sec:identification})---\emph{holds by structure}: \textbf{no experiment, ours or anyone's, could establish or refute this within an instructed design}. \textbf{(2)~Protocol---an identification audit an auditor can apply to a benchmark they did not build}, and \emph{a deliverable, not a by-product}: \textbf{five criteria} separating instruction-following from surface behavior from knowledge conflict from \emph{recovery of its own annotation mechanism} passing for detection, one of them unsupplied by any instructed design (Table~\ref{tab:criteria}). \textbf{(3)~Demonstration: the confound is not hypothetical, it is active.} Established across \textbf{eight models in four families}, two claim sets and \textbf{three detector paradigms}, and an audit of \textbf{both} public rollout releases finds that \textbf{no public release supplies all five} (§\ref{sec:experiments}, §\ref{sec:external_audit}; disclaimer~(2)). \textbf{(4)~A construct-valid benchmark: a positive fixed-elicitation control.} So we built materials that do---and there, \textbf{prior work's own battery separates realized deception at byte-identical elicitation} (pre-registered; §\ref{sec:external_audit}), reaching \textbf{rung~4 of Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}, not rung~5}. \textbf{The canonical benchmark is non-identifying, and deception is nevertheless detectable under a valid fixed-elicitation design.} \textbf{Stated plainly}: we do not show that LLM deception cannot be detected---(4) shows the opposite---but that \textbf{the canonical instructed-lie benchmark cannot establish that its detector is detecting deception rather than behavior induced by the elicitation procedure}. The constraint is elementary but \emph{unenforced}---no benchmark we audit states requirement~(i)---so \textbf{the protocol and the audit, not the algebra, are what is new}.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.} White-box probes read deception from activations~\citep{zou2023representation, burns2022discovering, azaria2023internal}; the black-box line's closest instance---our primary audit target---is \citet{pacchiardi2023catch}, and \textbf{we audit both paradigms} (§\ref{sec:td_vs_fd}). Three concurrent evaluations~\citep{kretschmar2025liarsbench, cooney2026didyoulie, hopkins2026liedetectors} find lie detectors fail to \textbf{generalize} beyond the regime they were fit in. \textbf{That is a generalization question; ours is the prior one---an \emph{identification} problem, not a generalization problem: generalization can be perfect while identification still fails.} \textbf{Fixed elicitation is also not what we claim to introduce}---\citet{cooney2026didyoulie}'s trained organisms have it and go \emph{beyond} it, with $D$ \emph{set} rather than observed. \textbf{Our novelty is using it as the missing construct-validity criterion for reading instructed-benchmark accuracy}, and auditing whether public artifacts supply it---a gap \textbf{invisible in a benchmark's own output}, the reported accuracy being the same number whether or not the criterion holds. Appendix~\ref{app:related_work} places all five kinds---instructed-lie benchmarks, generalization studies, belief-verified model organisms, the public rollout corpora, and four adjacent constructs---against requirements~(i)--(v) (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:prior_work_criteria}).

We begin with prior work's detector rather than our own, so no artifact of our reimplementation can carry the argument. \textbf{One question organizes every result: does the reported accuracy survive the controls that would have to hold before it could be read as being about deception?}

{\small\noindent\textbf{The design that would settle the attribution question---stated in full, and then run.} A corpus that (i)~fixes the elicitation prompt, (ii)~grades $D$ independently of the condition label, (iii)~has \textbf{the same scenario independently realized under both behavioral outcomes}, (iv)~exposes only the channel a deployed detector could read, and (v)~grounds $D$ in evidence not definitionally equivalent to the detector's input---(i)--(v) operationalize criterion~4 (§\ref{sec:eval_controls}). We audit \textbf{both} public rollout releases---ten rollout sets and 35 further cells---and \textbf{none supplies all five}: (iii) is missing on the closest set of the first, and (v) on the second. \textbf{We therefore test the strongest alternative interpretation---that our critique merely reflects an inability to detect deception}: under a fixed-elicitation design satisfying (i)--(v), \textbf{the same detector battery separates realized deception on three of five targets} (pre-registered; §\ref{sec:external_audit}).\par}
