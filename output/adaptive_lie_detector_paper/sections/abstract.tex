\begin{abstract}
Behavioral safety benchmarks are validated by predictive accuracy, not by construct identification. We introduce an identification audit for such benchmarks, and show that the dominant LLM deception paradigm fails it. \textbf{We do not ask whether LLMs can detect deception; we ask whether current benchmarks provide evidence that they do.} The standard paradigm scores behavioral LLM deception detectors in one setting, \emph{instructed roleplay}: the model is told to lie, and the instruction that produces the deception also produces the compliance, so a detector can score high while measuring whether the model was \emph{instructed} to lie. \textbf{We show the conflation is \emph{active}, not hypothetical.} The reason is structural: \textbf{no experiment that intervenes only on the instruction can separate a detector response mediated by deception from one mediated by instruction-following}---both are descendants of that instruction, and neither pathway is independently manipulated or observed. A deception-attribution reading therefore needs $\tau_D$, the effect of \emph{deception}, which the identified $\tau_E$---the \emph{instruction}'s effect---neither bounds nor signs, so no gain in accuracy, scale, or detector sophistication resolves it. \textbf{This is an identification problem, not a generalization problem: generalization can be perfect while identification still fails.} We turn it into a falsifiable audit protocol: five criteria we propose as necessary before accuracy licenses a deception claim---three alternative-explanation diagnostics, one construct-validity test that no instructed design can supply, and one robustness test.

Reimplemented from scratch, the standard black-box behavioral detector reaches 81.7\% on instructed-lie trials and 53.7\% once both conditions receive identical prompts. A pre-registered replication on a disjoint claim set and probe bank then \textbf{leaves no detectable above-chance discrimination on any of the six targets} (97.0\%$\to$\textbf{43.3\%}), as does a white-box linear probe on residual activations in two model families, at 100.0\% instructed, at chance equalized, and matched by surface features (92.5--99.0\%). \textbf{The accuracy the benchmark is scored on is reachable with no learned features at all}: a parameter-free lexical rule reaches 69--80\%---\emph{surface-accessible accuracy}, not a decomposition of the detector's signal. We then audit both public rollout releases for the one criterion that removes the elicitation confound: \textbf{nine of ten rollout sets cannot supply it} for a deployed-observable rule; on the tenth our rule is \textbf{null} while \textbf{the corpus authors' own judge, run verbatim on the same visible text, does separate deception there} (AUROC 0.71/0.94), so the null is the instrument's, not the criterion's; scoring the \emph{hidden} scaffold instead reports $+84.3$\,pp---\textbf{annotation-channel leakage}, the model echoing ``incorrect'' from its own instruction---so a benchmark must expose only the channel a deployed detector could read. A second release---35 cells, five generator families, fully pre-registered---fails differently: its label grades the very text both instruments read, so passing is \textbf{construct recovery}. We claim identification, not attribution: these detectors \emph{can} classify prompted deception and we reproduce it, and \textbf{equalization may remove the deception with the confound}. Because \textbf{no public release we audited supplies all five}, we built materials that do: with $D$ graded from a channel the detector never reads, prior work's battery separates realized deception on three of five targets---all three with power ($p\!\leq\!0.006$; two nulls underpowered)---of which \textbf{a blinded re-run replicates two, so the standing count is two of five}, with \textbf{one of five \emph{new} targets positive}. \textbf{The canonical benchmark is non-identifying, and a deception-\emph{associated} signal is nevertheless detectable under a valid fixed-elicitation design}---on \emph{some} targets, still not $\tau_D$---\textbf{rung~4 of five}, on the ladder the protocol defines. \textbf{Scope: English, instructed-roleplay evaluations, open-weight models 3B--70B.}
\end{abstract}
