\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Behavioral safety benchmarks are validated by predictive accuracy, not by construct identification. We introduce an identification audit for such benchmarks, and show that the dominant LLM deception paradigm fails it.} \textbf{We do not ask whether LLMs can detect deception; we ask whether current benchmarks provide evidence that they do.} The standard paradigm scores behavioral LLM deception detectors in one setting, \emph{instructed roleplay}: the model is told to lie, and the instruction that produces the deception also produces the compliance, so a detector can score high while measuring whether the model was \emph{instructed} to lie. \textbf{Accuracy measured this way cannot, by itself, establish that a detector responds to deception rather than to behaviors induced by the deception instruction}---and we show the conflation is \emph{active}, not hypothetical. The reason is an identification failure: such a benchmark identifies $\tau_E$, the \emph{instruction}'s effect on the detector, whereas detecting deception \emph{as such} is a claim about $\tau_D$, the effect of \emph{deception}---and $\tau_E$ neither bounds nor signs $\tau_D$, so \textbf{no increase in accuracy, model scale, or detector sophistication resolves it unless the evaluation introduces an intervention that separates deception from instruction-following.} \textbf{This is an identification problem, not a generalization problem: generalization can be perfect while identification still fails.} We turn it into a \textbf{falsifiable audit protocol}: five criteria required before accuracy licenses a deception claim---three alternative-explanation diagnostics, one construct-validity test that \textbf{no instructed design can supply}, and one robustness test.

Reimplemented from scratch, the standard black-box behavioral detector reaches 81.7\% on instructed-lie trials and \textbf{53.7\%} once both conditions receive identical prompts. A pre-registered replication on a disjoint claim set and probe bank then \textbf{eliminates above-chance discrimination for all six targets} (97.0\%$\to$\textbf{43.3\%}), two mechanistically distinct detectors failing under one control---as does \textbf{a white-box linear probe on residual activations in two model families}, at 100.0\% instructed but transferring to equalized responses at chance and matched by surface features (92.5--99.0\%). \textbf{Much of the benchmark's discriminative performance needs no learned features at all}: a parameter-free lexical rule reaches \textbf{69--80\%}---\emph{surface-accessible accuracy}, not a decomposition of the detector's signal. We then audit \textbf{both public rollout releases} for the one criterion that removes the elicitation confound: \textbf{nine of ten rollout sets cannot supply it} for a deployed-observable rule; on the tenth our rule is \textbf{null} ($+4.1$/$+7.7$\,pp) while \textbf{the corpus authors' own judge, run verbatim on the same visible text, does separate deception there} (AUROC 0.71/0.94, $p\!=\!0.0001$), so the null is the instrument's, not the criterion's; scoring the \emph{hidden} scaffold instead reports $+84.3$\,pp---\textbf{annotation-channel leakage}, the model echoing ``incorrect'' from its own instruction---so \textbf{a benchmark must expose only the channel a deployed detector could read}. \textbf{A second release---35 cells, five generator families, fully pre-registered---fails differently}: its label grades the very text both instruments read, so passing is \textbf{construct recovery}. We claim identification, not attribution: these detectors \emph{can} classify prompted deception and we reproduce it, and \textbf{equalization may remove the deception with the confound}. Because \textbf{no public release supplies all five}, we built materials that do: with $D$ graded from a channel the detector never reads, prior work's battery \textbf{separates realized deception on three of five targets}---all three with power ($p\!\leq\!0.006$; two nulls underpowered), \textbf{two of the three surviving a blinded re-run}. \textbf{The canonical benchmark is non-identifying, and deception is nevertheless detectable under a valid fixed-elicitation design}---deception-\emph{associated}, still not $\tau_D$. \textbf{Scope: English, instructed-roleplay evaluations, open-weight models 3B--70B.}
\end{abstract}
