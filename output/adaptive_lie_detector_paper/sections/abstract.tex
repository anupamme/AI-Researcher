\begin{abstract}
We show that high behavioral lie-detection accuracy in instructed LLM roleplay does not establish deception-specific detection. Our contribution is not a new detector: it is a \textbf{causal evaluation protocol} for determining whether a behavioral deception detector measures deception rather than the instruction to deceive. A from-scratch reimplementation of the published mechanism of Pacchiardi et al.\ (fixed unrelated yes/no elicitation questions, binary-coded answers, logistic regression) reproduces performance in the same broad range across six open-weight targets from 3B to 32B (70.0--100.0\% on five of six), yet prompt-equalizing the truth and deception conditions drops four of those five to chance (48--52\%, $p\!\geq\!0.468$; pooled $81.7\%\!\to\!53.7\%$). The same diagnostic collapses our own behavioral-feature pipeline 30--41\,pp. Equalization alone is ambiguous --- it might simply remove deception --- so a $2\!\times\!2$ factorial independently varies truth-value and deception instruction (§\ref{sec:factorial}). Holding truth-value fixed, the detector's response to the \emph{instruction} is large and consistently signed ($\beta_E\!=\!+1.3$ to $+1.6$\,SD, $p\!<\!0.001$) while its response to claim \emph{content} changes sign. Since the instruction also produces the deception, this is an \emph{identification} failure, not a demonstrated detector failure; grouped-by-claim cross-validation confirms the same picture for the binary-probe LR. Current instructed-roleplay benchmarks therefore conflate instruction-induced behavior with deception, and our experiments do not establish that their reported accuracy is deception-specific. Generalization is correspondingly weak: leave-one-model-out \textbf{64.2\%} [CI: 59--69\%] and leave-one-claim-pair-out \textbf{67.4\%} [CI: 64.6--70.3\%]. A hand-specified refusal-count regex reaches 69--80\% depending on pattern choice, bracketing the pipeline and showing the residual is largely reachable from surface form rather than establishing deception-specific representation; restricting to the one human-validated feature ($\alpha\!=\!0.606$) yields only \textbf{54.5\%} [CI: 40--67\%]. The Qwen~2.5~14B exception (70.0\% under equalization, $p\!=\!0.015$) is consistent with a single confidence-probe dimension tracking knowledge conflict. \textbf{Scope: English, instructed-roleplay evaluations, open-weight models 3B--70B.}
\end{abstract}
